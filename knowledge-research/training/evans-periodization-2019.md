# Periodization for Hypertrophy & Strength: Periodized > Non-Periodized; LP ≈ DUP

**Full citation:** Evans JW. Periodized Resistance Training for Enhancing Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength: A Mini-Review. Front Physiol. 2019;10:13. doi:10.3389/fphys.2019.00013. PMC6351492.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6351492/
**Access:** open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
Mini-review synthesizing meta-analyses on linear periodization (LP) vs daily undulating periodization (DUP) for strength and hypertrophy, addressing whether periodization is superior to non-periodized training.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Periodized vs non-periodized for strength:** Multiple meta-analyses demonstrate periodized RT is **superior to non-periodized** for maximal strength, consistently across volumes and experience levels.
- **LP vs DUP for strength:**
  - Harries et al. (2015): No significant difference; slight trend favoring UP for leg press.
  - Caldas et al. (2016): UP produced significantly greater strength gains than LP.
  - Williams et al. (2017): UP superior to LP for strength.
- **LP vs DUP for hypertrophy:** Two meta-analyses found **no hypertrophy advantage for either model** in untrained populations; few studies in trained individuals.
- Combining varied loading zones (2–4 RM + 8–12 RM + 20–30 RM) showed effect sizes favoring UP but without statistical significance.
- Key limitation: Superior periodized outcomes may reflect specificity (higher pre-test intensities) rather than variation per se.
- Periodization functions to improve adherence, enable constant progression, avoid plateaus, and reduce injury risk.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Periodized RT [is] superior to non-periodized RT for enhancing maximal strength."

> "Both program types yield similar results" for hypertrophy (periodized vs non-periodized).

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Any structured periodization beats random or non-progressive training for strength.
- For hypertrophy, the specific periodization model (LP vs DUP) matters less than having a structured plan.
- Rotating rep ranges (e.g., 4–6 heavy / 8–12 moderate / 15–20 light phases) is well-supported and provides variety.
- Deload weeks and unloading cycles should be incorporated — not just as rest but as structured low-volume phases.
- Users who are training to failure every session with no structure will plateau faster than those following a periodized plan.
