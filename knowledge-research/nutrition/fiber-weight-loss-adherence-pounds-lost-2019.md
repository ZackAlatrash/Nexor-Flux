# POUNDS Lost Study: Fiber Predicts Weight Loss and Adherence (Miketinas 2019)

**Full citation:** Miketinas DC, Bray GA, Beyl RA, Ryan DH, Sacks FM, Champagne CM. Fiber Intake Predicts Weight Loss and Dietary Adherence in Adults Consuming Calorie-Restricted Diets: The POUNDS Lost Study. J Nutr. 2019;149(10):1742–1748. doi:10.1093/jn/nxz117. PMC6768815.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6768815/
**Access:** Open access

## What this source covers
Secondary analysis of the POUNDS Lost RCT (345 participants, 6 months, 750 kcal/day restriction) examining fiber as a predictor of weight loss and dietary adherence across four different macronutrient compositions.

## Key findings & numbers
- Fiber was **the most influential predictor** of weight loss (partial R² = 0.17) among all dietary variables tested.
- An average increase of **3.7 g/day** dietary fiber predicted **1.4 kg additional weight loss** over 6 months (β = −0.37, p<0.0001).
- Mean baseline fiber intake: **17.5 g/day** → 6-month mean: **21.2 g/day** (+3.7 g/day).
- Only **48.7%** of participants met the 20 g/day fiber target at follow-up.
- Participants meeting the Adequate Intake for fiber: **2.94× higher odds** of diet adherence (99% CI: 1.56–5.55; p<0.0001).
- Adherent participants consumed more fiber: **25.2 g/d vs. 21.1 g/d** in non-adherent (effect size d = 0.72).
- Weight loss by fiber intake quartile:
  - Q1 (lowest fiber change): −5.8 kg
  - Q2: −5.8 kg
  - Q3: −7.1 kg
  - Q4 (highest fiber increase, +8–29 g/day): **−10.3 kg** (significantly greater than Q1–3, p<0.01)
- Sample: 345 participants; mean age 52.5±8.7 years; mean BMI 32.6±3.9 kg/m²; mean 6-month weight loss −7.27 kg.

## Mechanisms cited
1. Viscous fibers increase digesta viscosity, delay gastric emptying → greater satiety.
2. Fiber blunts digestibility of protein, total fat, and saturated fats → increased fecal energy excretion.
3. Microbiome changes affecting energy harvest from macronutrients.
4. Lower energy density of high-fiber foods prevents overconsumption.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Fiber is a proxy for diet quality AND a direct predictor of adherence — recommend high-fiber foods explicitly.
- IOM Adequate Intake: 25 g/day women, 38 g/day men (14 g per 1,000 kcal consumed).
- A 3–4 g/day increase in fiber is associated with meaningful additional weight loss; even modest fiber increases matter.
- Frame fiber not just as "healthy eating" but as an adherence tool: users who eat more fiber stick to their calorie goals better.
- High-fiber foods to emphasize: vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fruits (foods dense in satiety-promoting viscous fibers).
