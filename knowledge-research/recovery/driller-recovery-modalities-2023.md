# Recovery Modalities Review: Sleep and Nutrition Are the Foundation — Devices Are the Icing

**Full citation:** Driller M, Leabeater A. Fundamentals or icing on top of the cake? A narrative review of recovery strategies and devices for athletes. *Sports (Basel)*. 2023;11(11):213. doi:10.3390/sports11110213  
**PMCID:** PMC10674277  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10674277/  
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
A narrative review comparing fundamental vs. novel recovery strategies for athletes, including sleep, nutrition, periodisation, foam rolling, compression garments, cold water immersion (CWI), active recovery, massage guns, recovery boots, and photobiomodulation (PBMT). Distinguishes what has strong meta-analytic support from emerging/hyped modalities.

## Key findings & numbers

**Fundamental recovery ("the cake") — strong evidence:**
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h recommended per night; sleep loss impairs cognition, immunity, and athletic performance; described as the most powerful recovery tool
- **Nutrition:** Three pillars: (i) refuelling (carbohydrates), (ii) rebuilding (protein), (iii) rehydrating; all three must be addressed post-exercise
- **Periodisation:** Structured training manipulation across micro/meso/macrocycles essential for adaptation; the deload/taper is a fundamental recovery element

**Recovery devices ("the icing") — evidence summary:**
- **Foam rolling:** Optimal dose **90–120 seconds per muscle group**; reduces DOMS; improves range of motion; limited evidence for performance gains
- **Compression garments:** Most effective >24 hours post-resistance exercise; "71% of elite athletes sleep in compression garments at least once per week"; consistently reduces perceived muscle soreness
- **Cold water immersion (CWI):** Recommended **11–15°C for 11–15 minutes**; caution — chronic use after resistance training may "blunt physiological signals" and "disrupt the post-training inflammation process" necessary for hypertrophy
- **Active recovery:** Light-to-moderate exercise "considerably below the lactate threshold" reduces soreness perception; mixed evidence for performance outcomes
- **Photobiomodulation (PBMT):** Meta-analyses show positive findings for reducing fatigue; high risk of bias in individual studies
- **Massage guns:** Limited evidence; 2 case studies reported serious injuries (rhabdomyolysis, haemothorax)
- **Infrared sauna:** Early promise for neuromuscular recovery; conventional sauna has inconsistent effects

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Athletes may overlook some of the more well-established methods of recovery in favour of new or novel recovery technologies."

> "Cold water immersion...may disrupt the post-training inflammation process and blunt physiological signals" — caution with chronic use after resistance training.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- The hierarchy is clear: sleep → nutrition → periodisation → then devices; the coach should always address fundamentals before recommending gadgets
- CWI after strength sessions may blunt hypertrophy — relevant for recomposition: use CWI sparingly, not immediately post-strength training
- Foam rolling 90–120 s/muscle group is an accessible, evidence-grounded DOMS management tool to recommend
- Compression garments for soreness are well-evidenced and low-risk — a good recommendation for post-heavy-training nights
- Active recovery (easy walk, light cycling) is the best "I'm sore, what do I do?" answer for most users
