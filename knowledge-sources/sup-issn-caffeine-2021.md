## Is caffeine worth taking before training — dose and timing
tags: caffeine, "pre-workout", preworkout, dose, "how-much", timing, stimulant, supplement
Evidence strength: STRONG (one of the best-evidenced ergogenic aids; ISSN and AIS both classify it as Group/Category A).

**Dose:** 3–6 mg/kg body weight. For an 80 kg person that is 240–480 mg. A minimal effective dose may be as low as 2 mg/kg (~160 mg), so even a single strong cup of coffee (150–200 mg) provides benefit. Doses ≥9 mg/kg add no further benefit and markedly increase side effects.

**Timing:** approximately 60 minutes before exercise. Caffeine gum and mouth rinses may act faster (10–20 minutes) due to buccal absorption.

**What it helps:** aerobic endurance sees the most consistent moderate-to-large benefit (time-trial and time-to-exhaustion). Smaller but real benefits also exist for muscular endurance, movement velocity, muscular strength, sprinting, and jumping.

**FDA upper limit:** 400 mg/day for healthy adults.

## Caffeine tolerance and sleep — practical warnings
tags: caffeine, coffee, sleep, "energy-drink", stimulant, "pre-workout", timing, safe
Evidence strength: STRONG.

**Sleep:** caffeine half-life is approximately 5–6 hours. Taking 300 mg at 4 pm still leaves ~75 mg circulating at 10 pm. ISSN recommends the last dose be ≥8 hours before bedtime. Training after 4–5 pm? Use a lower dose or skip caffeine.

**Tolerance:** regular caffeine users experience attenuated ergogenic effects. A 7-day abstinence period can restore sensitivity — optional but effective before a key event. Habitual intake does not completely eliminate the benefit, but reduces its magnitude.

**Individual variation:** genetic differences in CYP1A2 and ADORA2A mean some people are "non-responders" or are especially sensitive to side effects (jitteriness, anxiety, GI upset). Start at the low end of the dose range (2–3 mg/kg) to assess personal response.
