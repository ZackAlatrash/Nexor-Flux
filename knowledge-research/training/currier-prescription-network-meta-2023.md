# Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis: Higher-Load, Multiset, ≥2×/Week Is Optimal

**Full citation:** Currier BS, McLeod JC, et al. Resistance training prescription for muscle strength and hypertrophy in healthy adults: a systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis. Br J Sports Med. 2023. doi:10.1136/bjsports-2023-106807. PMID:37414459. PMC10579494.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10579494/
**Access:** open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
Bayesian network meta-analysis of 178 strength studies (n=5,097; 45% women) and 119 hypertrophy studies (n=3,364; 47% women), comparing 12 resistance training prescriptions across sets, frequency, and load.

## Key findings & numbers
- **For muscle strength:** Higher-load, multiset, **3×/week** training was top-ranked — SMD 1.60 (95% CI 1.38–1.82) vs control. "95% probability that higher-load, multiset programs caused the largest strength gains."
- **For muscle hypertrophy:** Higher-load, multiset, **2×/week** training was top-ranked — SMD 0.66 (95% CI 0.47–0.85) vs control. "86.9% probability of ranking in top three for hypertrophy."
- "All resistance training prescriptions were superior to control for muscle strength and hypertrophy" — even low-volume, low-frequency.
- "95% probability that RT with at least **two sets or two sessions per week** increased strength."
- Load was less influential than volume for hypertrophy.
- 45–47% women representation — results are generalizable across sexes.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Minimum viable prescription: **2 sets × 2 sessions/week** per muscle group provides near-certain strength gains.
- Optimal for strength: higher load, multiset, 3×/week.
- Optimal for hypertrophy: higher load, multiset, 2×/week (but 3×/week is fine too).
- Confirms that "something is always better than nothing" — very important framing for adherence-focused coaching.
- Large, well-powered study with sex-balanced samples; findings are widely applicable.
