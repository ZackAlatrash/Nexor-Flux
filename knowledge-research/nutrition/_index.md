## Noted Conflicts Between Sources

1. **Protein upper limit for muscle gain:** Morton 2018 (meta-analysis, resistance-training-with-surplus context) places the break point at 1.62 g/kg/day with a practical ceiling of ~2.2 g/kg/day. ISSN position stands (Jäger 2017, Helms 2014) recommend 2.3–3.1 g/kg LBM during a calorie **deficit** to preserve lean mass. These are not contradictory — the higher range applies specifically to hypocaloric conditions where protein also serves anti-catabolic purposes, not to muscle-building surpluses.

2. **Per-meal anabolic ceiling:** Older literature suggested 20–25 g per meal maximized MPS. Areta 2013 showed 20 g every 3 hours was superior to both 10 g/1.5 h and 40 g/6 h. Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018 challenged the 20–25 g ceiling with evidence that 40 g produces ~20% more myofibrillar MPS in isolation. The practical resolution: aim for 20–40 g per meal; larger meals with slower-digesting proteins or mixed macronutrient context can utilize more than 25 g.

3. **Ketogenic diet efficacy:** Aragon 2017 (ISSN) concludes that keto shows no fat loss advantage when calories and protein are equated. The keto advantage under ad libitum conditions is ~294 kcal/day spontaneous intake reduction (appetite suppression) — which is real but not a metabolic magic effect.
