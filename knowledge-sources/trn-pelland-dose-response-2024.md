## Diminishing returns: how much volume is too much?
tags: volume, sets, "sets-per-week", hypertrophy, strength, dose, gains, muscle, "how-much"

More weekly sets produce more muscle and strength — but with sharply diminishing returns beyond roughly 20 direct sets per muscle per week. This meta-regression of 67 studies (2,058 participants) showed that both hypertrophy and strength gains increase with volume, yet strength hits diminishing returns faster and harder than hypertrophy does. Practically: going from 5 → 10 sets/week yields a large jump in results; going from 15 → 20 sets/week still helps but less so; going beyond 20 sets/week is hard to justify for most natural trainees. For body recomposition, target 10–20 direct sets per muscle group per week to stay in the productive zone without accumulating unnecessary fatigue.

## Count direct sets, not total sets — indirect volume counts less
tags: volume, sets, hypertrophy, strength, muscle, exercise, selection, "how-many"

Not all sets are equal. This analysis distinguishes **direct sets** (the muscle is a primary mover, e.g., biceps curls for biceps) from **indirect sets** (the muscle is a secondary helper, e.g., rows for biceps). Counting indirect volume equally with direct volume overestimates effective training dose. When tracking weekly sets per muscle, count compound movements that directly target the muscle at full value, and give partial credit (roughly 0.5) to exercises where the muscle assists. For example: bench press counts fully for chest and triceps, partially for front delts; overhead press counts fully for shoulders and triceps, partially for chest. This distinction is essential for accurately judging whether you are training each muscle with enough stimulus.
