# AIS Sports Supplement Framework — ABCD Classification (2022)

**Full citation:** Australian Institute of Sport. AIS Sports Supplement Framework. Australian Sports Commission. Updated August 2022.
**Source URL:** https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements/about-the-ais-sports-supplement-framework
**Access:** open access (public government resource)

## What this source covers
The AIS (Australian Institute of Sport) Sports Supplement Framework provides an independent, evidence-based classification of sports supplements, foods, and ergogenic aids into four groups (A, B, C, D) based on scientific evidence, legality, safety, and practical considerations. Originally developed from the AIS Sports Supplement Program (2000–2013); latest position statement August 2022.

## Key findings & numbers

### Group A — Strong Evidence; Approved for Use in Specific Situations
Criterion: "Strong scientific evidence for use in specific situations in sport using evidence-based protocols; permitted for use by identified athletes according to best practice protocols."

**Sports Foods:**
- Sports drinks (carbohydrate + electrolyte; used during/after exercise)
- Sports gels (65–75 g/100 mL carbohydrate; concentrated energy)
- Sports confectionery (concentrated carbohydrate in jelly form)
- Sports bars (compact carbohydrate for pre/intra-exercise)
- Electrolyte supplements (sodium, potassium replacement)
- Isolated protein supplements (whey, casein, soy; ~15–20% of total body mass guidance)
- Mixed macronutrient supplements (bars, powders, liquid meals)

**Medical Supplements:**
- Calcium, Folate, Iron, Vitamin D, Zinc (for diagnosed deficiency or special needs)

**Performance Supplements (Group A):**
- Caffeine
- Beta-alanine
- Dietary nitrate / beetroot juice
- Sodium bicarbonate
- Creatine monohydrate
- Glycerol (for hyperhydration in heat)

### Group B — Emerging Evidence; Research Continues
Criterion: Limited or emerging evidence; may be provided to athletes in research or clinical contexts pending further evidence.
- Includes antioxidants in specific contexts, collagen/gelatin + vitamin C, HMB, quercetin, and other compounds under investigation

### Group C — No Evidence; Not Recommended
Criterion: No meaningful evidence of benefit; not recommended.
- Includes most herbal supplements, proprietary blends, many weight-loss supplements

### Group D — Banned / High Contamination Risk
Criterion: Contains ingredients on the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) prohibited list, or at high risk of contamination with prohibited substances.
- Includes prohormones, stimulants such as ephedra, SARMs

### Multi-Ingredient Supplement Caution
The framework cautions against proprietary blend products citing risks of ineffective doses, harmful ingredient interactions, and contamination from multiple sourcing locations.

## Direct specifics / quotes
(Paraphrased from official framework document, August 2022)
> Group A supplements have "strong scientific evidence for use in specific situations in sport using evidence-based protocols."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Evidence strength: provides an authoritative, independent cross-reference classification consistent with ISSN position stands
- Group A = the only supplements a recomp coaching app should proactively recommend: creatine, caffeine, beta-alanine, sodium bicarbonate, dietary nitrate, protein supplements, and sports foods
- Group B = can discuss if user asks, but with caveats about limited evidence
- Group D = clearly flag as banned/high-risk; never recommend
- The AIS framework is respected internationally and provides credibility when citing supplement recommendations
