# HRV in Strength and Conditioning: RMSSD as a Practical Recovery Biomarker

**Full citation:** Addleman JS, Lackey NS, DeBlauw JA, Hajduczok AG. Heart rate variability applications in strength and conditioning: A narrative review. *J Funct Morphol Kinesiol*. 2024;9(2):93. doi:10.3390/jfmk9020093  
**PMCID:** PMC11204851  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11204851/  
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
A narrative review of HRV measurement, interpretation, and practical application in strength and conditioning contexts, covering training adaptation, recovery monitoring, overtraining detection, and field-based protocols.

## Key findings & numbers
- **HRV definition:** Fluctuation of time intervals between adjacent heartbeats; a surrogate measure of autonomic nervous system (ANS) function
- **Best metric:** RMSSD (root mean square of successive differences) — "more sensitive and less affected by respiratory rate" than frequency-domain measures; best reflects parasympathetic activity
- **Endurance adaptation:** Night-time RMSSD significantly increases following well-structured endurance training programs — indicating improved recovery capacity
- **Resistance training:** HRV research in resistance training is limited; associations with adaptation are less established than in endurance sports
- **Recovery monitoring protocol:**
  1. Athlete establishes personal baseline RMSSD over 2–4 weeks
  2. Define "smallest worthwhile change" (SWC) window around baseline
  3. When RMSSD is **within SWC** → train as normal at moderate-to-high intensity
  4. When RMSSD is **below SWC** → modify to lower intensity or full recovery day
- **Overtraining indicator:** Prolonged reduction in HRV associated with increasing training load; high training volume reduces both HRV and subsequent performance
- **Key principle:** Relative differences to individual baseline are more meaningful than population norms — absolute RMSSD values vary widely between individuals
- Reductions in RMSSD associated with: high training load, incomplete recovery, and autonomic stress
- Increases in RMSSD associated with: rest, reduced training load, parasympathetic rebound

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Relative differences to an individual's own baseline are the best interpretive level" rather than population norms.

> "When an athlete's HRV is inside their SWC, they train as normal with moderate to high intensity."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- HRV tracking (via wearable) can be represented in the app through the energy score (1–10) as a subjective proxy
- A consistent downtrend in energy scores over 5–7 days is analogous to suppressed HRV — trigger for a deload recommendation
- HRV-guided training is the gold standard for individualising recovery; the app should acknowledge this if users ask about wearables
- The "baseline + SWC" framework is directly applicable: if energy score drops below personal average for 3+ days → reduce training intensity, not volume first
- Post-workout HRV suppression that normalises within 24 h = normal FOR; suppression lasting >48 h = signal to back off
