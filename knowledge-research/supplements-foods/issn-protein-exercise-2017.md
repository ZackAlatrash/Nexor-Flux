# ISSN Position Stand: Protein and Exercise (2017)

**Full citation:** Jäger R, Kerksick CM, Campbell BI, et al. International Society of Sports Nutrition Position Stand: protein and exercise. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2017;14:20. doi: 10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8. PMID: 28642676
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477153/
**Access:** open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
Comprehensive ISSN position stand on dietary protein requirements for exercising individuals, covering total daily intake, per-meal dosing, protein timing, protein quality (including leucine content, EAA profiles, PDCAAS/DIAAS), and comparison of protein sources (whey, casein, egg, beef, soy, plant proteins).

## Key findings & numbers
- Daily protein intake (maintenance/muscle building): 1.4–2.0 g/kg body weight/day for physically active individuals
- During energy restriction: 2.3–3.1 g/kg/day to preserve lean mass
- During energy restriction in resistance-trained individuals: >3.0 g/kg/day may further promote fat loss
- Per-meal optimal dose: 0.25 g protein/kg body weight; absolute amount 20–40 g per serving
- Elderly individuals: 40 g per serving to maximally stimulate MPS (due to reduced anabolic sensitivity)
- Meal distribution: every 3–4 hours throughout the day to maximize MPS
- Pre-sleep protein: 30–40 g casein 30 minutes before sleep increases overnight MPS without impairing lipolysis
- Leucine per meal: 1–3 g optimal; 700–3000 mg recommended per acute protein dose
- Essential amino acids (EAA) per dose: 6–15 g to stimulate MPS
- Anabolic window: MPS remains elevated for up to 24–72 hours post-exercise; the narrow 30-min post-workout window is not supported
- Protein quality rankings (highest to lowest): whey (11% leucine) > casein (9.3% leucine) > egg > beef > soy > plant proteins
- Plant protein note: soy inhibits mTOR via phytoestrogens; pea, rice, hemp have incomplete or lower EAA content
- MPS peak signaling: 30–60 minutes post-exercise; elevation persists up to 24 hours

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "For building muscle mass and for maintaining muscle mass through a positive muscle protein balance, an overall daily protein intake in the range of 1.4–2.0 g protein/kg body weight/day is sufficient for most exercising individuals."

> "Ingestion of essential amino acids (EAA; approximately 10 g) either in free form or as part of a protein bolus of approximately 20–40 g has been shown to maximally stimulate muscle protein synthesis (MPS)."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Evidence strength: STRONG (extensive RCT and meta-analysis base)
- Primary coaching numbers: 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day as practical target; 20–40 g per meal; leucine-rich sources preferred
- Whey protein is the highest quality supplement source due to leucine content and fast absorption
- Pre-sleep casein (30–40 g) is a validated strategy for overnight recovery
- For plant-based users: need 20–40% more plant protein to match animal protein EAA delivery; combine complementary sources
- The 30-minute post-workout window is not as narrow as previously believed; focus on total daily intake first
