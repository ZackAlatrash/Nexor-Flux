## Protein target while cutting — lean body mass based
tags: protein, cut, cutting, deficit, lose, loss, lean, muscle, macros, how-much
When in a calorie deficit, protein needs are higher than for maintenance or surplus. The evidence-based target is **2.3–3.1 g/kg of lean body mass per day** (use LBM, not total body weight, especially for individuals with higher body fat). In one study, subjects eating only 1.0 g/kg lost 1.6 kg of lean mass in 2 weeks; at 2.3 g/kg with the same deficit they lost only 0.3 kg. Go toward the upper end of 2.3–3.1 as leanness increases — leaner individuals have less fat to burn and more muscle at risk. Roberts 2020 extends this to **up to 3.5 g/kg for hunger control** during aggressive cuts.

## How fast should you lose weight — deficit and rate
tags: weight, rate, fat, loss, lose, cut, cutting, lean, muscle, sustainable, scale, deficit, calories
Target **0.5–1% of body weight per week** (Helms 2014). Physique athletes who lost at ≤0.5%/week placed better in competitions than those losing faster (Roberts 2020). A slower group losing 0.7%/week lost 31% of their fat mass and actually gained 2.1% lean mass over the diet; a faster group (1.4%/week) lost only 21% of fat mass with no lean gain. In practice: a 70 kg person targets 350–700 g/week of weight loss. Diets lasting 2–4+ months at this rate yield better lean mass retention than aggressive crash diets.

## Fat intake floor during a cut — don't go too low
tags: fat, dietary-fat, cut, cutting, deficit, calories, hormones, macros, how-much
Do not drop dietary fat below **15% of total calories** (Helms 2014) or **10% as an absolute minimum** (Roberts 2020). Very low fat intake reduces testosterone, though the primary hormonal driver is caloric deficit and body fat loss, not fat percentage per se. At very low fat intakes, essential fatty acid and fat-soluble vitamin needs may go unmet. A practical rule: set protein first, set fat at 15–25% of calories, use the remaining calories for carbohydrate.

## Carbohydrate during a cut — fill in after protein and fat
tags: carbs, carb, cut, cutting, deficit, fuel, calories, calorie, muscle, energy
Carbohydrate is the flexible residual macro: set protein (2.3–3.1 g/kg LBM) and fat (15–25% kcal) first, then fill remaining calories with carbohydrate. Carbs serve as primary fuel for resistance training, so keeping them as high as possible within the calorie budget protects training performance and helps retain muscle. Target **2–5 g/kg/day** carbohydrate for physique athletes in a cut (Roberts 2020). Cutting carbs below training energy demands is one of the most common and avoidable errors during prep.

## NEAT suppression during dieting — the hidden calorie drain
tags: calories, calorie, tdee, plateau, metabolic-adaptation, weight, fat, loss, deficit
Non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT) — fidgeting, walking, daily movement — can vary by up to **2,000 kcal/day** between individuals. A 10% reduction in body weight causes roughly **262 kcal/day** of NEAT suppression; 20% weight loss causes ~500 kcal/day. This involuntary energy compensation is a primary reason fat loss stalls. Users who report strict adherence but plateau are likely experiencing NEAT suppression, not cheating. Calorie targets may need to be revised downward as the diet progresses.
