# Training Frequency & Strength: Volume Equated = No Frequency Difference

**Full citation:** Ralston GW, Kilgore L, Wyatt FB, Buchan D, Baker JS. Weekly Training Frequency Effects on Strength Gain: A Meta-Analysis. Sports Med Open. 2018;4(1):36. doi:10.1186/s40798-018-0149-9. PMC6081873.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6081873/
**Access:** open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
Meta-analysis of 12 studies (299 subjects) examining how weekly training frequency (1–3+ days per week) affects strength gains over 8–24 week training periods.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Non-volume-equated comparison (1× vs ≥3×/week):**
  - Low frequency (1×/week): ES = 0.71
  - High frequency (≥3×/week): ES = 0.78
  - Difference: not statistically significant (p = 0.25)
- **Medium vs High frequency:** Small significant effect (ES 0.31; 95% CI 0.05–0.58; p = 0.02)
- **Upper body strength:** Low vs high frequency significant difference (ES 0.48; p < 0.01)
- **Lower body strength:** No significant difference between frequencies (p = 0.22)
- **Volume-equated:** Low vs high frequency ES = 0.03 (95% CI −0.20–0.27); p = 0.78 — trivial, no significance.
- 12 studies; 299 subjects; training durations 8–24 weeks; rep ranges 3–15/set.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "No significant effect exists between low and high training frequency when volume is equated."

> "Higher frequency appears beneficial primarily because it increases total weekly volume rather than frequency itself being the causal factor."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Same conclusion as hypertrophy research: frequency is a tool to accumulate volume, not a direct strength driver.
- Upper body strength gains appear somewhat more frequency-sensitive than lower body.
- Recommend 2–3×/week per muscle as practical guideline; what matters is hitting total weekly set targets.
- Users who can only train 1–2×/week can achieve comparable strength gains if volume is maintained.
