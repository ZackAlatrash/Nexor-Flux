# Progressive Overload: Load Increases and Rep Increases Are Both Viable Strategies

**Full citation:** Plotkin D, Coleman M, Van Every D, Maldonado J, Oberlin DJ, Israetel M, Feather J, Alto A, Vigotsky AD, Schoenfeld BJ. Progressive overload without progressing load? The effects of load or repetition progression on muscular adaptations. PeerJ. 2022;10:e14142. doi:10.7717/peerj.14142. PMC9528903.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528903/
**Access:** open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
8-week RCT in 43 resistance-trained participants (27 men, 16 women; mean age 23.1 years) comparing two progressive overload strategies: increasing load (LOAD group) vs increasing repetitions (REPS group) for lower body hypertrophy and strength.

## Key findings & numbers
- Both groups gained **appreciable muscle mass: 6.7–12.9%** across measurement sites.
- **Hypertrophy:** Rectus femoris modestly favored REPS group by 2.8 mm (95% CI −0.5 to 5.8 mm); other muscles showed negligible between-group effects (<1 mm differences).
- **Strength (1RM squat):** LOAD group showed slight advantage of 2.0 kg (95% CI −2.4 to 7.8 kg); both groups gained ~20 kg average — difference "of questionable practical significance."
- **Muscular endurance:** Negligible differences; REPS performed 1.02× more repetitions.
- **Countermovement jump:** No meaningful changes in either group (0.1 cm difference).
- Progressive overload principle: increasing neuromuscular demand over time facilitates further adaptations.
- Either form of progression keeps the stimulus novel and effective.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Both progressions of repetitions and load appear to be viable strategies" for muscle development over 8 weeks.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Users don't need to add weight every session — adding reps within a target range is an equally valid progression.
- This validates a "double progression" scheme: first add reps to the top of a range, then increase load.
- Example protocol: target 8–12 reps; when you hit 12 on all sets, increase load and drop back to 8.
- Either form of progression signals overload to the muscle; the key is that SOMETHING progresses over time.
- Tracking both weight AND reps gives users more flexibility and prevents stagnation.
