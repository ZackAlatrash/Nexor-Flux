# Sleep Restriction Drives Late-Night Eating and Weight Gain

**Full citation:** Spaeth AM, Dinges DF, Goel N. Effects of experimental sleep restriction on weight gain, caloric intake, and meal timing in healthy adults. *Sleep*. 2013;36(7):981-990. doi:10.5665/sleep.2792  
**PubMed:** PMID 23814334  
**Source URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23814334/  
**Access:** Abstract-only (full text via Oxford Academic)

## What this source covers
A large controlled experiment (N = 225 total; N = 37 with detailed diet tracking) in a University of Pennsylvania sleep laboratory examining the effects of 5-night sleep restriction on weight gain, caloric intake, and the timing of eating within the day.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Sleep design:** 2 baseline nights (10 h sleep) → 5 sleep restriction nights (4 h/night, 04:00–08:00) → 2 recovery nights; vs. control condition (10 h/night throughout)
- **Weight gain:** Sleep-restricted subjects gained significantly **more weight** than controls (effect size d = 0.51)
- **Extra calories consumed during sleep restriction:** **+552.9 ± 265.8 additional calories** during the late-night window (22:00–03:59) across the restriction period
- Total daily caloric intake increased due to more meals eaten per day and extra late-night snacking
- **Fat intake during late night (22:00–03:59): 33.0%** of calories vs. daytime 28.2% and evening 29.4% — sleep-restricted individuals ate fattier food at night
- **Demographic variation:** African American subjects gained more weight than Caucasian subjects under sleep restriction (d = 0.37); males gained more than females (d = 0.38)
- This study demonstrates that the extra eating is concentrated in the late-night window — likely due to extended wakefulness providing more opportunity and lower inhibitory control

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Sleep-restricted subjects gained significantly more weight than controls."

> "Increased daily caloric intake was due to more meals and the consumption of 552.9 ± 265.8 additional calories between 22:00 and 03:59."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Sleep-restricted individuals overeat by over 550 kcal specifically during late-night hours — coach should address meal timing and sleep simultaneously
- When a user reports sleeping late or waking up frequently, the coach should anticipate and address late-night snacking risk
- The higher fat percentage of late-night eating compounds poor body composition outcomes
- Weight tracking combined with sleep duration logging allows the coach to identify this pattern ("you've gained 0.5 kg this week and averaged 5.5 h sleep — this may be related")
- Encouraging earlier bedtimes eliminates the late-night eating window entirely
