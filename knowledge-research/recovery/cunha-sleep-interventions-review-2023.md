# Sleep Extension and Napping Are the Most Effective Sleep Interventions for Athletes

**Full citation:** Cunha LA, Costa JA, Marques EA, Brito J, Lastella M, Figueiredo P. The impact of sleep interventions on athletic performance: A systematic review. *Sports Med Open*. 2023;9:58. doi:10.1186/s40798-023-00599-z  
**PMCID:** PMC10354314  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10354314/  
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
Systematic review of 25 intervention studies (2011–2021) across multiple sleep intervention types in athletic populations, assessing effects on sleep quality, duration, and physical/cognitive performance.

## Key findings & numbers
- **25 studies included:** 60% crossover trials, 8% RCTs, 32% non-RCTs; search in PubMed, SPORTDiscus, Web of Science (May 2022)
- **Sleep extension:** All included studies showed **positive performance improvements**; extending by **46–113 minutes** in athletes who habitually slept ~7 h/night was identified as a practical recommendation range for extension programs
- **Napping:** A **20–90-minute nap** can improve performance outcomes after a regular night of sleep AND restore performance decrements to baseline after partial sleep restriction
- **Sleep hygiene** (consistent schedule, screen curfews, cool/dark environment): "had **no effects** on sleep and subsequent performance/recovery" — though based on only a few studies
- **Light manipulation** (bright morning light, blue-light blocking in evening): "demonstrated promising results, but more studies are needed"
- **Electronic device removal at night:** No significant effects observed
- **Mindfulness:** "showed promising results" but requires sustained (≥8 week) implementation to see benefits
- Authors emphasise: more RCTs with standardised methodology are needed

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Extending sleep duration by 46–113 min...over 3–49 nights in athletes that habitually slept ~7 h per night may be a logical recommendation for future sleep extension programs."

> "A 20–90-min nap can improve performance outcomes after a regular night and restore performance decrements to baseline levels after a night with partial sleep restriction."

> "It appears that increasing sleep duration at night or through napping was the most effective interventions to improve physical and/or cognitive performance."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- **Sleep extension is the #1 evidence-based sleep intervention** — even adding 46 min/night has meaningful effects; the coach should recommend gradual earlier bedtimes
- Napping 20–90 min is a practical same-day fix when the user reports last night was poor — distinguish from longer naps which may disrupt night sleep
- Sleep hygiene advice (no screens, consistent schedule) is standard but has weaker direct performance evidence; still include as habit scaffolding
- Mindfulness for sleep needs weeks — appropriate as a slow-burn recommendation for chronically stressed users
- The lack of RCTs means uncertainty about mechanisms, but pragmatic recommendations remain clear: more sleep = better
