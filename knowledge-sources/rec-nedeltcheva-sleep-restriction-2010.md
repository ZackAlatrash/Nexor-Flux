## Sleep restriction destroys fat loss even when calories are controlled

tags: sleep, fat, loss, muscle, weight-loss

In a randomised crossover trial, overweight adults spent 14 days on a 680 kcal/day deficit under either 5.5 hours or 8.5 hours of sleep per night. The total weight lost was the same in both conditions (~3 kg). But what was lost differed dramatically: those sleeping 5.5 hours lost **55% less of their weight as fat** and **60% more as lean (muscle) mass** compared to the 8.5-hour group.

This is the most important sleep finding for anyone on a cut: **not sleeping enough doesn't just slow fat loss — it actively causes muscle wasting, even when protein and calories are on track.** You can eat perfectly and still lose mostly muscle if you're consistently under 7 hours. For recomposition goals, sleep duration belongs alongside calorie and protein targets as a non-negotiable.

## Sleep deprivation raises hunger and ghrelin during a calorie deficit

tags: sleep, appetite, hunger, cravings, tired, low-energy

In the same Nedeltcheva trial, cutting sleep to 5.5 hours elevated hunger ratings and circulating ghrelin (the hunger hormone) despite identical caloric intake in both conditions. Sleep-restricted subjects also felt more fatigued and less satisfied. This is a physiological response — not a willpower failure. When you're in a deficit and sleeping poorly, your body is hormonally signalling for more food. Prioritising 7–9 hours of sleep during a cut makes adherence significantly easier because it keeps appetite hormones in check.
