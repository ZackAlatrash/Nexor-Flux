# ISSN Position Stand: Nutrient Timing (2017)

**Full citation:** Kerksick CM, Arent S, Schoenfeld BJ, Stout JR, et al. International society of sports nutrition position stand: nutrient timing. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2017;14:33. doi: 10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4. PMID: 28919842
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5596471/
**Access:** open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
ISSN position stand on nutrient timing for healthy exercising adults, covering pre-exercise, intra-exercise, and post-exercise macronutrient strategies for both endurance and resistance training, plus pre-sleep nutrition. Received July 2017, accepted August 2017.

## Key findings & numbers

### Pre-Exercise Nutrition
- Pre-endurance meal carbohydrate: 1–4 g/kg consumed several hours before high-intensity (≥70% VO2max) exercise >90 min
- Carbohydrate within 30 min before exercise: increased exercise capacity in some protocols
- Final meal window: within 4 hours of exercise start for glycogen maintenance

### Intra-Exercise Nutrition
- During exercise carbohydrate (endurance): ~30–60 g/hour in 6–8% carbohydrate-electrolyte solution every 10–15 min
- Intra-exercise carbohydrate ceiling: up to 90 g/hour with glucose+fructose blends
- Daily carbohydrate for high-volume training: 8–12 g/kg/day

### Post-Exercise Nutrition (Glycogen)
- Rapid glycogen restoration: 0.6–1.0 g/kg body mass within first 30 min, then every 2 hours for 4–6 hours
- Aggressive carbohydrate refeeding: 1.2 g/kg/hour over 3.5 hours
- Protein + carbohydrate for glycogen: 0.8 g/kg/hour carbohydrate + 0.2–0.4 g/kg/hour protein during recovery

### Post-Exercise Protein / Anabolic Window
- Anabolic window duration: muscles remain anabolically sensitized for at least 24 hours post-exercise (not a 30-min window)
- MPS elevation: serum amino acids and MPS elevated for 3–5 hours post-feeding
- Muscle full effect: MPS returns to baseline in ~90 min despite continued elevated serum amino acids
- Optimal dose: 20–40 g (0.25–0.40 g/kg body mass) high-quality protein every 3–4 hours

### Pre-Sleep Nutrition
- Casein dose: 30–40 g ingested 30 min before sleep
- Effects: increases overnight MPS; maintains overnight lipolysis and fat oxidation; elevates morning resting metabolic rate vs. placebo

### EAA Dosing
- EAA dose: approximately 10 g (either in free form or as part of a 20–40 g protein bolus) maximally stimulates MPS

### Key Position on Total Daily Intake
- Meeting total daily protein intake with evenly spaced feedings (every ~3 hours) is the primary priority over window-based timing

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Meeting the total daily intake of protein, preferably with evenly spaced protein feedings (approximately every 3 h during the day), should be viewed as a primary area of emphasis for athletes focused on body composition."

> "Consuming casein protein (~ 30–40 g) prior to sleep can acutely increase MPS and metabolic rate throughout the night without influencing lipolysis."

> "Ingesting a 20–40 g protein dose (0.25–0.40 g/kg body mass/dose) of a high-quality source every three to 4 h appears to most favorably affect MPS rates."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Evidence strength: STRONG for daily total protein and distribution; MODERATE for specific window timing
- The "anabolic window" is 24 hours, not 30 minutes — total daily protein matters most
- Pre-sleep casein is a validated strategy: recommend 30–40 g of cottage cheese, casein powder, or Greek yogurt before bed
- Post-workout carbohydrate timing is most critical for same-day double training sessions; once-daily trainers have more flexibility
- Practical recommendation: 20–40 g protein per meal, every 3–4 hours, total 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day
