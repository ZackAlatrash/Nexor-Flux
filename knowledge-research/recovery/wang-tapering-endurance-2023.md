# Tapering 41–60% Volume for 8–14 Days Maximises Endurance Performance

**Full citation:** Wang Z, Wang Y, Gao W, Zhong Y. Effects of tapering on performance in endurance athletes: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *PLoS One*. 2023;18(5):e0282838. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0282838  
**PMCID:** PMC10171681  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10171681/  
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
A systematic review and meta-analysis of 14 studies (174 endurance athletes — runners and cyclists) quantifying the effects of tapering strategies on time-trial (TT) and time-to-exhaustion (TTE) performance, examining optimal volume reduction, duration, and the role of pre-taper overload training.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Time-trial performance:** Significant improvement post-taper (SMD = −0.45; p < 0.05)
- **Time to exhaustion:** Large performance gains post-taper (SMD = 1.28; p < 0.05)
- **VO₂max and running economy:** No significant change after tapering (p > 0.05) — performance gains come from fatigue dissipation, not aerobic capacity increases
- **Optimal volume reduction:**
  - **41–60% reduction** → largest TT effect (SMD = −0.77; p < 0.05)
  - ≤40% reduction: smaller effect
  - ≥60% reduction: smaller effect (detraining risk)
  - Best practice: reduce volume by ~50% while maintaining **training intensity and frequency**
- **Optimal taper duration:**
  - **8–14 days** → maximum benefit (SMD = −1.47; p < 0.05)
  - 15–21 days also significant but smaller effect
  - ≤7 days: significant but lower effect size than 8–14 days
- **Progressive vs. step taper:** A progressive reduction (gradual week-by-week) was identified as effective
- **Pre-taper overload:** Tapering after a pre-taper overload training block produced significantly greater performance gains than conventional tapering alone (p < 0.05)
- A ≤21-day taper overall is the practical upper limit recommendation

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "A ≤21-day taper, in which training volume is progressively reduced by 41–60% without changing training intensity or frequency, is an effective tapering strategy."

> "Tapering applied in conjunction with pre-taper overload training seems to be more conducive to maximize performance gains."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- **Deload protocol:** Reduce weekly training volume by ~50% every 4–8 weeks of progressive overload, while keeping intensity (weight on the bar) the same
- The 8–14 day window maps well to a standard 1-week deload recommendation — well within optimal range
- Performance is NOT lost during a 1-week deload: VO₂max and strength are maintained; fatigue is cleared, revealing actual fitness
- Users who fear deloads "losing gains" can be reassured: the evidence shows performance improves post-taper
- For users training for a specific event (race, competition, photo shoot), a planned 1–2 week taper with 50% volume cut is the evidence-based approach
- Tracking training load over 4–6 week blocks allows the coach to identify when a deload is due
