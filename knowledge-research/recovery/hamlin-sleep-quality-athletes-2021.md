# Athletes Sleeping ≥8 Hours Have 20% Lower Injury/Illness Risk and Better Training Quality

**Full citation:** Hamlin MJ, Deuchrass RW, Olsen PD, Choukri MA, Marshall HC, Lizamore CA, Leong C, Elliot CA. The effect of sleep quality and quantity on athlete's health and perceived training quality. *Front Sports Act Living*. 2021;3:705650. doi:10.3389/fspor.2021.705650  
**PMCID:** PMC8461238  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8461238/  
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
A longitudinal monitoring study of 82 university athletes (55 male, 27 female; ages 18–23) tracked across an academic year via daily mobile-app self-reporting, quantifying relationships between sleep duration/quality and health outcomes (injury, illness, mood, energy, training quality).

## Key findings & numbers
- Athletes averaged **8.2 ± 1.0 hours/night** (males) and **8.2 ± 1.1 hours/night** (females)
- **81% of recorded nights** involved ≥8 hours sleep (79% male; 82% female)
- **Before university start:** 100% of nights were ≥8 hours; by week 3 of semester: only **62%** — stress-related decline
- Sleep quality (5-point Likert): peak 3.8 ± 0.4 pre-semester; maintained ~3.5 during term

**Benefits of sleeping ≥8 hours (odds ratios):**
- Improved mood: **OR = 1.3** (95% CI: 1.1–1.5)
- Better energy levels: **OR = 1.2** (95% CI: 1.0–1.4)
- Improved perceived training quality: **OR = 1.3** (95% CI: 1.1–1.5)
- **Reduced injury/illness odds: OR = 0.8** (95% CI: 0.7–0.9) — **20% lower risk**

**Sleep quality additional benefit:**
- Higher sleep quality (Likert ≥3) associated with **40% lower injury/illness risk: OR = 0.6** (95% CI: 0.5–0.7)
- Sleep quality predicted adequate duration: OR = 2.9 for achieving ≥8 h on nights with good quality

**Stress mechanism:**
- Sleep disruption is a "commonly reported symptom of increased stress in athletes particularly through increased training workload"
- Three stress types observed: dual-burden (sport + academics), sport-specific, and conditional stress

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Athletes sleeping ≥8 hours exhibited improved mood markers (OR = 1.3) and higher energy levels (OR = 1.2) compared to those sleeping <8 hours."

> "Higher sleep quality demonstrated even stronger protective effects: 40% risk reduction (OR = 0.6, 95% CI 0.5–0.7)."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- **20–40% injury risk reduction from adequate sleep** is a compelling, concrete benefit to communicate to users
- Sleep quality matters as much as quantity: users who report 8 h but wake frequently or feel unrefreshed may still be at elevated risk
- The academic-year disruption pattern illustrates that life stress drives sleep decline — the coach should ask about life stressors, not just training load
- Energy score (1–10) in the app functions as a simplified Hooper-style wellbeing check — validated by this study's methodology
- Stress → poor sleep → reduced training quality is a predictable cascade; early stress detection in check-ins can prompt sleep prioritisation before performance suffers
- The 62% compliance at week 3 of a stressful period suggests habit erosion is fast — the coach should reinforce sleep habits during stressful life periods
