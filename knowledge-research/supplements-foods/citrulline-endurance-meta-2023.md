# Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis: Citrulline and Endurance Performance (2023)

**Full citation:** Harnden CS, Agu J, Gascoyne T. Effects of citrulline on endurance performance in young healthy adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2023;20(1):2209056. doi: 10.1080/15502783.2023.2209056
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10167868/
**Access:** open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 9 RCTs examining L-citrulline supplementation for endurance performance (cycling and running) in young healthy adults. Addresses time-to-exhaustion (TTE), time-to-completion (TTC), and VO2max.

## Key findings & numbers
- Studies included: 9 RCTs; 158 participants total
- Doses studied: 1.47–9 g L-citrulline
- Loading protocols: 7–16 days supplementation; bolus 60–120 minutes pre-exercise
- Exercise modalities: cycling (6 studies), running (3 studies)
- Time-to-exhaustion (TTE) pooled effect: SMD = 0.03 [95% CI: −0.27, 0.33]; p = 0.83 (no significant effect)
- Time-to-completion (TTC) pooled effect: SMD = −0.07 [95% CI: −0.39, 0.26]; p = 0.67 (no significant effect)
- VO2max: no significant changes reported across studies
- Individual studies did not demonstrate consistent ergogenic effects even with higher doses
- Heterogeneity across studies was moderate

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "The current evidence suggests no significant benefit of citrulline supplementation for endurance performance."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Evidence strength: LOW for endurance performance; L-citrulline does not improve aerobic time-trial or time-to-exhaustion performance
- Combined with the citrulline malate review (2021): evidence overall for citrulline (as CM or L-cit) is limited and inconsistent
- Coaching recommendation: do not recommend citrulline primarily for endurance performance; at best, may support muscular endurance in resistance exercise (weak evidence)
- The popular "pump" effect reported by gym-goers may be real but is not consistently captured by performance metrics
- Safe supplement with no documented side effects but evidence does not support ergogenic claims for endurance
