# Elite Athletes Sleep < 7 Hours; Sleep Restriction Increases Injury Risk 1.7-Fold

**Full citation:** Hatia M, Loureiro N, Ribeiro J, Moeda F, Melo M, Tocha J, Schonenberger A, Correia C. A narrative review of the impact of sleep on athletes: sleep restriction causes and consequences, monitoring, and interventions. *Cureus*. 2024;16(12):e76635. doi:10.7759/cureus.76635  
**PMCID:** PMC11779686  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11779686/  
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
A narrative review covering the prevalence, causes, and consequences of sleep restriction in elite athletes, methods for monitoring sleep, and evidence-based interventions — providing a practical clinical overview for practitioners.

## Key findings & numbers

**Prevalence of poor sleep in athletes:**
- Elite athletes averaged **6.5 hours of sleep** measured by actigraphy (objective measurement)
- **39.1%** reported inadequate sleep (< 7 hours)
- Athletes felt they got insufficient sleep on average **3.8 nights per week**
- **19.8%–50%** exhibited poor sleep quality scores

**Performance consequences:**
- Sleep restriction decreased "endurance, muscular strength, and power"
- After a **single night of four-hour sleep restriction**: average AND maximum power output during Wingate anaerobic cycle tests declined significantly
- Cognitive function (reaction time, decision-making) impaired alongside physical performance

**Injury risk:**
- Adolescent athletes sleeping **< 8 hours/night were 1.7 times more likely to sustain an injury** compared to those achieving adequate rest

**Primary causes of poor sleep in athletes:**
- Competition timing (late evening games/events)
- Congested competition/travel schedules
- Travel across time zones (jet lag)
- Psychological stress (competitive anxiety, academic pressure)
- Medical conditions: **sleep-disordered breathing affects 14% of professional football players** vs. 4% of general population

**Monitoring methods (evidence-ranked):**
1. Polysomnography — gold standard but impractical for field use
2. Actigraphy (wrist-worn motion tracker) — practical for ongoing monitoring
3. Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) — validated questionnaire
4. Hooper Index — wellness questionnaire (sleep, fatigue, stress, muscle soreness) — simple and widely used in sport

**Evidence-based interventions:**
- Sleep extension (nightly + strategic napping 20–90 min)
- Sleep hygiene practices (consistent schedule, cool/dark room, no screens 1 h before bed)
- Bright light therapy (morning exposure to reset circadian rhythm)
- Melatonin (0.5–5 mg) preferred over benzodiazepines for pharmacological support
- Avoid caffeine within 6 hours of bedtime

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- 39% of elite athletes sleep inadequately — recreational athletes likely even higher; this is a universal problem, not an edge case
- The 1.7× injury risk from sleeping < 8 h makes sleep a safety recommendation, not just a performance optimiser
- The Hooper Index (sleep, fatigue, stress, soreness) is essentially what the app already tracks — validate to users that this is an established monitoring protocol
- Single night of 4-h sleep → measurable power reduction: coach should advise against PR attempts or heavy training after poor sleep
- Sleep-disordered breathing (snoring, OSA) may be relevant if users report consistently poor sleep despite adequate hours — worth flagging to consult a doctor
