## What is HRV and what does it tell you about recovery

tags: hrv, monitoring, readiness, stress, fatigue, tired, recovery, overtraining

Heart rate variability (HRV) is the fluctuation in time between consecutive heartbeats — a proxy for autonomic nervous system (ANS) function. The most useful metric is **RMSSD** (root mean square of successive differences), which reflects parasympathetic (rest-and-recovery) activity and is less sensitive to breathing rate than frequency-domain measures. Higher RMSSD = better parasympathetic tone = better recovered. Lower RMSSD = more physiological stress = less ready to train hard.

HRV is not meaningful in isolation — it must be interpreted **relative to your own personal baseline**, not population norms. Individual values vary widely. After 2–4 weeks of daily measurements, you establish your baseline and a "smallest worthwhile change" (SWC) window around it. When your RMSSD is inside the SWC, train at moderate-to-high intensity as planned. When it drops below the SWC, reduce intensity or take a recovery day. When it rises above, you may be primed for a hard session.

## How to use HRV (or energy scores) to guide training intensity

tags: hrv, monitoring, readiness, fatigue, tired, deload, recovery, overtraining

In practice, few recreational athletes have a validated HRV monitoring protocol. The energy score (1–10) tracked daily in this app functions as a subjective proxy for the same signal: it captures perceived recovery capacity and correlates with autonomic state.

Apply the same principle: track your baseline over 2–4 weeks, then respond to deviations:
- Energy score at or above your normal range → train hard as planned
- Energy score below baseline for **1–2 days** → reduce session intensity, don't push through
- Energy score below baseline for **3+ consecutive days** → consider a deload or rest day; this mirrors the suppressed HRV pattern associated with insufficient recovery or early overreaching

Post-workout HRV suppression that normalises within 24 hours reflects normal functional overreaching. Suppression lasting >48 hours signals inadequate recovery from that session. With a consistent monitoring habit, these patterns become clear over 4–8 weeks of data — allowing the coach to give intensity recommendations grounded in your individual physiology rather than generic schedules.
