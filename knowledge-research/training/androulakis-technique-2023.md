# Training Technique for Hypertrophy: Full ROM + Long Muscle Length Emphasis Is Key

**Full citation:** Androulakis Korakakis P, Wolf M, Coleman M, Burke R, Piñero A, Nippard J, Schoenfeld BJ. Optimizing Resistance Training Technique to Maximize Muscle Hypertrophy: A Narrative Review. J Funct Morphol Kinesiol. 2024;9(1):9. doi:10.3390/jfmk9010009. PMC10801605.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10801605/
**Access:** open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
Narrative review (with Schoenfeld as co-author) examining how technique variables — range of motion, tempo, muscle length — influence hypertrophic adaptations.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Repetition tempo:** Optimal range is **2–8 seconds per repetition**. "Significant increases in hypertrophy can occur with repetition durations between 2 and 8s." Evidence is unclear whether extending eccentric or concentric phase preferentially enhances growth.
- **Range of motion (ROM):** Training should "employ a ROM that allows for muscles to be fully stretched." Full ROM is the baseline recommendation.
- **Muscle length priority:** "Training at long muscle lengths elicits more distal hypertrophy than training at short muscle lengths." A partial ROM at the **stretched/lengthened position** may be superior to full ROM.
- **Long muscle length advantage:** Multiple studies show "partial ROM performed at longer muscle lengths was potentially superior to full ROM for hypertrophy" — especially for quadriceps, biceps, and triceps.
- Evidence on strict vs non-strict form effects on hypertrophy is minimal — most recommendations derive from biomechanics rather than controlled hypertrophy studies.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Training at long muscle lengths elicits more distal hypertrophy than training at short muscle lengths."

> "A partial ROM performed at longer muscle lengths was potentially superior to a full ROM for hypertrophy."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Emphasize the "stretch" portion of exercises for hypertrophy: deep squats, full ROM curls, deficit deadlifts, fully stretched lat pulldowns.
- Coach tip: prioritize exercises/rep ranges that take the muscle to full stretch under load (e.g., Romanian deadlifts vs leg curls with short ROM).
- Tempo: 2–3s eccentrics are practical and supported; no need for extreme slow-tempo protocols.
- "Long muscle length" training may explain why incline curls, deep squats, and Romanian deadlifts tend to produce superior hypertrophy in practice.
- When users ask about partial reps: clarify that partials at the LENGTHENED position may be beneficial, but partials at the shortened position are counterproductive.
