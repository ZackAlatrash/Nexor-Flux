## How much beta-alanine should I take and does it work
tags: "beta-alanine", carnosine, dose, "how-much", supplement, "worth-it", endurance, buffering
Evidence strength: STRONG for high-intensity exercise lasting 1–4 minutes; limited for longer endurance or pure strength work (AIS Group A / ISSN Category A).

**Dose:** 4–6 g/day, split into multiple doses of ≤2 g each. Do not take one large 4–6 g dose — boluses >800 mg cause paresthesia (tingling). Sustained-release capsules allow larger single doses without tingling.

**Duration before effect:** minimum 2–4 weeks of daily dosing. Muscle carnosine rises ~20–30% after 2 weeks, ~40–60% after 4 weeks, and up to 80% after 10 weeks.

**Best exercise type:** high-intensity efforts lasting 1–4 minutes — 400–1500 m runs, rowing 500 m–2000 m, HIIT intervals, combat sports rounds. Physical working capacity at fatigue threshold improves 14–20%. Modest benefit up to ~25 minutes; very limited evidence beyond that.

**Mechanism:** beta-alanine combines with histidine in muscle to form carnosine, a intramuscular pH buffer that delays hydrogen ion (H⁺) accumulation and fatigue during glycolytic work.

## The tingling from beta-alanine — what it is and how to reduce it
tags: "beta-alanine", tingles, paresthesia, dose, supplement, safe, "pre-workout"
Evidence strength: N/A (this is a known pharmacological effect, not a safety concern).

Beta-alanine causes a flushing/tingling sensation (paresthesia) on the skin — most noticeable on the face, ears, and arms — within 20–30 minutes of taking a dose >800 mg as a standard (non-SR) formulation. It is completely benign and resolves within 60–90 minutes. No tissue damage occurs.

**To reduce or eliminate tingling:**
1. Split doses into ≤1.6 g per serving, spread 3–4 times through the day.
2. Take with food.
3. Use a sustained-release (SR) formulation — same daily dose, much less tingling.

Many pre-workout supplements include 1.6–3.2 g beta-alanine per serving; if you take one scoop you may feel tingling. With consistent daily use some individuals report reduced sensitivity over time.
