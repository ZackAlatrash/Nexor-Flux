## How close to failure should you train for muscle growth? (RIR for hypertrophy)
tags: failure, rir, "reps-in-reserve", effort, hypertrophy, muscle, growth, gains, hard, "how-many"

Proximity to failure matters for muscle growth but not in an all-or-nothing way. This meta-regression found a clear, meaningful dose-response: the closer each set comes to failure, the more hypertrophy it produces (the slope for estimated RIR was consistently negative and its confidence interval excluded zero). The practical target is **0–3 reps in reserve (RIR)** per set — meaning you stop when you feel you have 0–3 reps left. A companion systematic review (Refalo et al. 2022, 15 studies) confirmed that training to absolute momentary muscular failure adds only a trivial advantage (ES = 0.12, p = 0.34) over non-failure training for hypertrophy, so grinding to true failure every set is unnecessary and increases injury risk and recovery costs. Stop 1–2 reps short of failure on most working sets; reserve absolute failure for occasional "all-out" sets on low-risk exercises (e.g., cables, machines).

## RIR for strength vs muscle growth — different rules apply
tags: failure, rir, "reps-in-reserve", effort, strength, hypertrophy, muscle, hard, load

Proximity to failure has very different effects depending on your goal. For **strength**, this meta-regression found a negligible dose-response — you can leave 3–5 reps in reserve and still maximise strength gains. For **hypertrophy**, the dose-response is meaningful and closer to failure is consistently better. This means: if your current goal is body recomposition or muscle growth, push sets to 1–2 RIR regularly; if your primary goal is 1RM strength, you can train more conservatively (3–5 RIR) on heavy compound lifts without sacrificing strength gains, which also reduces injury risk during near-maximal loading.
