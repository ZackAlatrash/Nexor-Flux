# ISSN Position Stand: Protein and Exercise (Jäger 2017)

**Full citation:** Jäger R, Kerksick CM, Campbell BI, et al. International Society of Sports Nutrition Position Stand: protein and exercise. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2017;14:20. doi:10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8. PMID: 28642676. PMC5477153.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477153/
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
A comprehensive ISSN position stand reviewing protein intake for healthy exercising individuals, covering daily dose, per-meal dosing, timing, protein quality, leucine thresholds, and protein source comparisons.

## Key findings & numbers
- Daily protein for exercising individuals: **1.4–2.0 g/kg/day** is sufficient for building and maintaining muscle mass.
- During caloric restriction (hypocaloric conditions): **2.3–3.1 g/kg/day** to preserve lean body mass in resistance-trained subjects.
- Intakes exceeding **3.0 g/kg/day** may promote additional fat loss in resistance-trained individuals (emerging evidence).
- Optimal acute dose: **0.25 g/kg per meal, or an absolute dose of 20–40 g** of high-quality protein.
- Leucine content per dose: **700–3,000 mg leucine** plus a balanced EAA array to maximally stimulate MPS.
- Elderly individuals may need **~40 g per dose** (vs. ~20–25 g for young adults) to maximally stimulate MPS.
- Protein doses should be distributed **every 3–4 hours** across the day.
- Pre-sleep casein protein: **30–40 g** increases overnight MPS and metabolic rate without influencing lipolysis.
- Anabolic effect of exercise lasts **at least 24 hours**; immediate post-workout timing is less critical than daily totals.
- Whey leucine content: **11%** by mass; casein: **9.3%** by mass (both complete proteins with high PDCAAS).
- At doses >3.0 g/kg/day in resistance-trained individuals, protein may amplify fat loss via thermic and satiating effects.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "An overall daily protein intake in the range of 1.4–2.0 g protein/kg body weight/day (g/kg/d) is sufficient for most exercising individuals."

> "Acute protein doses should strive to contain 700–3000 mg of leucine and/or a higher relative leucine content, in addition to a balanced array of the essential amino acids (EAAs)."

> "Total protein and calorie intake appears to be the most important consideration when it comes to promoting positive adaptations to resistance training, and the impact of timing strategies (immediately before or immediately after) to heighten these adaptations in non-athletic populations appears to be minimal."

> "Pre-sleep casein protein intake (30–40 g) provides increases in overnight MPS and metabolic rate without influencing lipolysis."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Default protein target for active users: 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day; raise to 2.3–3.1 g/kg/day when in a calorie deficit.
- Advise spreading protein across meals every 3–4 hours; 20–40 g per meal is the practical target.
- Casein or cottage cheese before bed (30–40 g) is a validated strategy for overnight recovery.
- Total daily protein matters most; timing is secondary to hitting the daily target.
