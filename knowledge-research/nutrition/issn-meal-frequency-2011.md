# ISSN Position Stand: Meal Frequency (La Bounty 2011)

**Full citation:** La Bounty PM, Campbell BI, Wilson J, et al. International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: meal frequency. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2011;8:4. doi:10.1186/1550-2783-8-4. PMC3070624.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3070624/
**Access:** Open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
ISSN position stand reviewing the evidence on meal frequency (1–9 meals/day) and its effects on body composition, metabolic rate, hunger, blood markers, and protein metabolism in both sedentary and athletic populations.

## Key findings & numbers
- Increasing meal frequency does **not** significantly improve body composition in sedentary populations when total calories are equated.
- Weight loss is equivalent across meal frequencies (1–9 meals/day) when total calories are controlled: both 3-meal and 6-meal groups lost ~5% body weight with no significant lean/fat mass differences (Cameron et al. 2010).
- Increasing meal frequency does **not** significantly enhance diet-induced thermogenesis, total energy expenditure, or resting metabolic rate.
- **Hunger and satiety:** Increasing meal frequency **helps decrease hunger and improve appetite control**; single-meal group consumed 27% (~358 kcal) more at subsequent ad libitum meal vs. multiple-meal group (Speechly et al.).
- **Blood markers:** ~5% lower total and LDL cholesterol in individuals eating >6 times/day vs. 1–2 times/day (Titan et al. 2001).
- Serum insulin 27.9% lower with 17 snacks vs. 3 meals per day (Jenkins et al.).
- **Protein metabolism (athletes):** In boxers on 1,200 kcal/day hypocaloric diet, 6 meals/day caused significantly less lean body mass loss than 2 meals/day over 2 weeks (Iwao et al. 1996).
- Nitrogen balance: significantly reduced loss with 5 vs. 1 meal/day during hypocaloric intake (Garrow et al. 1981).
- Optimal protein per meal: **20–30 g high-quality protein** or **10–15 g essential amino acids** per eating occasion.
- Mixed muscle protein synthesis ~23% greater with three 850-calorie meals + three 180-calorie snacks (15 g EAAs each) vs. three meals alone (Paddon-Jones et al.).
- U.S. adults average: **3.47 meals/day**; elite athletes consume **5–10 eating occasions daily**.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Increasing meal frequency does not appear to favorably change body composition in sedentary populations."

> "Increasing meal frequency does not appear to significantly enhance diet induced thermogenesis, total energy expenditure or resting metabolic rate."

> "Increasing meal frequency appears to help decrease hunger and improve appetite control."

> "Increased meal frequency appears to have a positive effect on various blood markers of health, particularly LDL cholesterol, total cholesterol, and insulin."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Meal frequency is a tool for compliance and hunger management, not a metabolic booster; don't overclaim about "stoking metabolism."
- For resistance-trained users aiming to maximize MPS: 4–6 meals per day, each with 20–30 g protein, is the evidence-based optimum.
- For fat loss without a specific MPS goal: any frequency the user can sustain works; emphasize hitting daily totals.
- More frequent meals improve hunger control — a strong argument for recommending 4+ meals to users struggling with adherence.
