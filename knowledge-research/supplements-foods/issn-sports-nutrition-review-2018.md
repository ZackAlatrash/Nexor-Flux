# ISSN Exercise & Sports Nutrition Review Update (2018)

**Full citation:** Kerksick CM, Wilborn CD, Roberts MD, et al. ISSN exercise & sports nutrition review update: research & recommendations. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2018;15:38. doi: 10.1186/s12970-018-0242-y. PMID: 30068354
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6090881/
**Access:** open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
Broad update review of exercise and sports nutrition research covering macronutrient needs, weight management, supplement evidence tiers, and nutrient timing. Published August 2018. Companion to individual ISSN position stands; provides a synthesized three-tier classification for supplement efficacy.

## Key findings & numbers

### Three-Tier Supplement Evidence Framework

**Category A — Strong Evidence to Support Efficacy and Apparently Safe:**
Supplements with "a sound theoretical rationale with the majority of available research in relevant populations using appropriate dosing regimens demonstrating both its efficacy and safety."
- Creatine monohydrate (3–5 g/day maintenance; 20 g/day × 5 days loading)
- Caffeine (3–6 mg/kg body weight ~60 min pre-exercise)
- Sodium bicarbonate (0.3 g/kg ~60–180 min pre-exercise for high-intensity tasks)
- Beta-alanine (4–6 g/day in divided doses for 4+ weeks for high-intensity efforts 1–4 min)
- Protein supplements (when total daily protein targets cannot be met via food)
- Carbohydrate supplements (intra-workout for efforts >60–90 min)

**Category B — Limited or Mixed Evidence to Support Efficacy:**
Supplements with "a sound scientific rationale for its use, but the available research has failed to produce consistent outcomes supporting its efficacy."
- HMB (mixed results in trained athletes)
- Citrulline / citrulline malate
- BCAAs (leucine, isoleucine, valine)
- Beta-hydroxy-beta-methylbutyrate
- Nitrate/beetroot juice

**Category C — Little to No Evidence to Support Efficacy and/or Safety:**
Supplements that "generally lack a sound scientific rationale and the available research consistently shows it to lack efficacy."
- Glutamine (for muscle hypertrophy in healthy exercising adults)
- Tribulus terrestris
- Most thermogenics beyond caffeine
- Many proprietary blends

### Protein & Macronutrient Numbers
- Daily protein: 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day for muscle building; 2.3–3.1 g/kg/day during caloric restriction
- Carbohydrate during exercise: 30–60 g/hour for efforts >60–90 min
- Daily carbohydrate for high-volume training: 8–12 g/kg/day

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Category A: Strong Evidence to Support Efficacy and Apparently Safe — Supplements that have a sound theoretical rationale with the majority of available research in relevant populations using appropriate dosing regimens demonstrating both its efficacy and safety."

> "Category C: Little to No Evidence to Support Efficacy and/or Safety — Supplements within this category generally lack a sound scientific rationale and the available research consistently shows it to lack efficacy."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Evidence strength: provides the definitive tier-based framework for deciding which supplements to recommend
- Tier A supplements (creatine, caffeine, sodium bicarbonate, beta-alanine, protein) should be the only supplements proactively recommended in coaching
- Tier B supplements can be discussed if the user asks but should be presented with appropriate uncertainty
- Tier C supplements (glutamine for muscle, most herbal blends) should not be recommended for performance
- Protein and carbohydrate targets from this review are consistent with other ISSN position stands
