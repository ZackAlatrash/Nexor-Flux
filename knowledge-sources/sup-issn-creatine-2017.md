## How much creatine should I take — loading and maintenance dose
tags: creatine, "creatine-monohydrate", loading, dose, "how-much", maintenance, supplement
Evidence strength: STRONG (1,000+ studies; ISSN calls creatine "the most effective ergogenic nutritional supplement currently available").

**Maintenance (recommended for most people):** 3–5 g/day of creatine monohydrate indefinitely. Larger athletes (>90 kg) may need 5–10 g/day. No cycling required.

**Optional loading phase (for faster saturation):** 20 g/day (5 g × 4 doses) for 5–7 days, then drop to 3–5 g/day maintenance. Achieves a 20–40% increase in muscle creatine within one week. Slower alternative: 3 g/day for 28 days reaches the same endpoint without loading.

Creatine monohydrate is the best-studied and cheapest form. No need to buy "advanced" creatine forms (HCl, ethyl ester, etc.) — monohydrate outperforms them all in head-to-head research.

## Will creatine hurt my kidneys or make me gain fat
tags: creatine, "creatine-monohydrate", safe, safety, "worth-it", supplement, weight
Evidence strength: STRONG. Long-term use up to 30 g/day for 5 years documented as safe in healthy individuals with no evidence of renal harm.

The only expected side effect is a scale weight increase of ~1–2 kg in the first 1–2 weeks — this is intramuscular water retention and lean mass gain, not fat. Creatine does not cause dehydration, cramping, or musculoskeletal injury; studies show the opposite (lower injury rates vs. placebo). Individuals with pre-existing kidney disease should consult a doctor before use, but healthy adults have no concern.

## What does creatine actually do for muscle and strength
tags: creatine, creatine-monohydrate, strength, muscle, gains, mass, build
Evidence strength: STRONG. Creatine replenishes phosphocreatine (PCr), the primary fuel for very short, explosive efforts (ATP resynthesis during the first 10–30 seconds of high-intensity work).

In resistance training studies vs. placebo: 10–20% improvement in high-intensity and repetitive exercise performance, consistently greater strength gains and lean mass gains. Effect is most pronounced for multi-set resistance training, sprinting, and repeated-sprint sports. Less impact on single-rep max or aerobic endurance exercise.
