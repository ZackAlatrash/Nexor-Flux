# Umbrella Review: Evidence-Based RT Variables for Hypertrophy

**Full citation:** Bernárdez-Vázquez R, Raya-González J, Castillo D, Beato M. Resistance Training Variables for Optimization of Muscle Hypertrophy: An Umbrella Review. Front Sports Act Living. 2022;4:949021. doi:10.3389/fspor.2022.949021. PMC9302196.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9302196/
**Access:** open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
Umbrella review (review of systematic reviews/meta-analyses) synthesizing evidence on multiple resistance training variables and their effects on hypertrophy, providing consolidated evidence-based recommendations.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Volume:** At least **10 weekly sets per muscle group** to maximize hypertrophy; 2–3 sets per exercise minimum. Greater weekly volume beyond this did not yield consistent additional benefits.
- **Intensity (load):** Loads **≤60% 1RM** can produce "substantial increases in muscle hypertrophy" when approaching failure; loads >60% 1RM showed a modest trend toward greater adaptations.
- **Frequency:** When volume is equated, training frequency does NOT significantly impact hypertrophy. Frequency is a programming tool to manage weekly volume.
- **Contraction type:** Combine eccentric + concentric; eccentric training offers "small advantages" (~10% vs ~6.8% gains with concentric-only).
- **Rep duration (tempo):** Optimal range **0.5–8 seconds per repetition**; very slow reps (≥10 seconds) are "inferior from a hypertrophy standpoint."
- **Exercise order, time of day, periodization model:** These variables show NO direct influence on muscle mass gains — choose based on preference and adherence.
- **Blood Flow Restriction (BFR):** Beneficial when heavy loads cannot be used. Most effective with ≥8-week duration and cuff pressures >150 mmHg.
- Light OR heavy loads both effective when training approaches muscular failure.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- 10+ sets/week per muscle is the minimum threshold; 12–20 is the optimal zone (consistent with Baz-Valle 2022).
- Wide load range is acceptable (e.g., 60–85% 1RM) — gives flexibility without sacrificing results.
- Tempo should be controlled but not extremely slow; 2s concentric / 2–3s eccentric is practical.
- BFR flagged as useful for injured or limited users unable to use heavy loads.
- Eccentric emphasis (e.g., slow lowering) provides a small but real edge for hypertrophy.
