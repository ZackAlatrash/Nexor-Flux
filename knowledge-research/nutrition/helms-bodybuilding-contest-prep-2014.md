# Evidence-Based Recommendations for Natural Bodybuilding Contest Prep: Nutrition (Helms 2014)

**Full citation:** Helms ER, Aragon AA, Fitschen PJ. Evidence-based recommendations for natural bodybuilding contest preparation: nutrition and supplementation. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2014;11:20. doi:10.1186/1550-2783-11-20. PMID: 24864135. PMC4033492.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033492/
**Access:** Open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
Evidence-based guide for natural drug-free bodybuilders during contest prep, covering protein, fat, carbohydrate, deficit rate, meal frequency, nutrient timing, supplementation, peak week strategies, and psychological risks.

## Key findings & numbers
- Protein: **2.3–3.1 g/kg of lean body mass per day**; higher end appropriate as body fat decreases (leaner = more aggressive deficit risk = higher protein needed).
- Protein at 1.0 g/kg resulted in **1.6 kg lean mass loss** over 2 weeks vs. only **0.3 kg loss** at 2.3 g/kg (same caloric deficit).
- Weight loss rate: **0.5–1% of body weight per week** (diets longer than 2–4 months yield this rate).
- Slower group (0.7%/week) lost 31% fat mass and gained 2.1% lean mass vs. faster group (1.4%/week) losing 21% fat with no lean gain.
- Fat intake: **15–30% of total calories** (lower end 15–20% acceptable if needed to keep protein and carbs adequate).
- Testosterone decreases modestly when fat drops from 40% to 20% of calories — caloric deficit and body fat loss are the primary hormonal drivers, not fat percentage.
- Carbohydrate: remaining calories after protein and fat are set; emphasis on **preserving training performance**.
- Meal frequency: **3–6 meals per day**, each containing a minimum of **20 g protein**.
- Pre- and post-exercise protein: **0.4–0.5 g/kg body weight** at each peri-workout meal.
- Creatine monohydrate: **3 g/day for 28 days** achieves 20% muscle creatine increase (no loading phase needed for full saturation over time).
- Caffeine: **5–6 mg/kg** prior to exercise for performance enhancement; cycling may preserve ergogenic effect.
- Beta-alanine: **6.4 g/day for 4 weeks** increased muscle carnosine by **64.2%**; benefits exercise bouts 60–240+ seconds.
- Dehydration and electrolyte manipulation in peak week: **"may not improve appearance"** and **"have the potential to worsen it"**.
- Psychological: 46% of male drug-free bodybuilders reported binge eating after competitions; 81.5% reported preoccupation with food during prep.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Caloric intake should be set at a level that results in bodyweight losses of approximately 0.5 to 1%/wk to maximize muscle retention."

> "The lower the body fat of the individual, the greater the imposed caloric deficit and when the primary goal is to retain LBM, the higher the protein intake (within the range of 2.3-3.1 g/kg of LBM) should be."

> "Diets longer than two to four months yielding weight loss of approximately 0.5 to 1% of bodyweight weekly may be superior for LBM retention compared to shorter or more aggressive diets."

> "The global macronutrient composition of the diet is likely the most important nutritional variable related to chronic training adaptations."

> "At this time it is unknown whether dehydration or electrolyte manipulation improves physique appearance. What is known is that these practices are dangerous and have the potential to worsen it."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Default protein guidance during a cut: 2.3–3.1 g/kg LBM (use lean body mass, not total body weight).
- 0.5–1%/week loss rate is the cornerstone recommendation — use this to set calorie deficits.
- Fat should not go below 15% of total calories; protect hormonal health.
- Carbohydrates are the flexible residual — keep them as high as possible while hitting protein and fat floors.
- Meal frequency of 3–6 meals/day is practical and sufficiently distributed; more isn't necessarily better.
- Screen for psychological risks especially post-competition; disordered eating prevalence is high in this population.
