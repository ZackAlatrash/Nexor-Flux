# RIR Dose-Response: Proximity to Failure Matters More for Hypertrophy Than Strength

**Full citation:** Robinson ZP, Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Refalo MC, Jukic I, Steele J, Zourdos MC. Exploring the Dose-Response Relationship Between Estimated Resistance Training Proximity to Failure, Strength Gain, and Muscle Hypertrophy: A Series of Meta-Regressions. Sports Med. 2024. doi:10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2. Preprint: SportRxiv doi:10.51224/SRXIV.295.
**Source URL:** https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/295
**Access:** open access (SportRxiv preprint)

## What this source covers
Series of meta-regressions from Florida Atlantic University Muscle Physiology Lab examining the dose-response relationship between estimated reps in reserve (RIR) and both muscle hypertrophy and strength gains.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Strength outcomes:** Negligible relationship between proximity to failure and strength gains. "Confidence intervals of the marginal slopes for estimated RIR contained a null point estimate" across all strength models. Similar strength gains occur across a wide RIR range.
- **Hypertrophy outcomes:** Meaningful dose-response relationship — "marginal slopes for estimated RIR were negative and their confidence intervals did NOT contain a null point estimate." Muscle size increases as sets approach failure.
- Key contrast: **Proximity to failure matters MORE for hypertrophy than for strength**.
- For strength: conservative RIR (e.g., 3–5 RIR) is adequate; for hypertrophy: closer to failure (0–3 RIR) produces superior results.
- RIR was estimated (not directly measured) from intervention descriptions — a methodological limitation.
- Future studies needed with larger samples and directly-measured RIR.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Optimal proximity to failure may differ between strength and muscle hypertrophy outcomes."

> For hypertrophy: "marginal slopes for estimated RIR were negative and their confidence intervals did not contain a null point estimate" — meaning closer to failure = more growth.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- For hypertrophy-focused users: target **0–3 RIR per set** (stop when you feel you have 0–3 reps left in the tank).
- For strength-focused users: **3–5 RIR** is adequate; grinding to failure may increase injury risk without extra strength benefit.
- A recomp protocol should lean toward 1–2 RIR for hypertrophy with maintained technical form.
- Coach tip: "If you could have done 4+ more reps, the set may not be effective for muscle growth."
- Pairs well with Refalo 2022 finding that MMF is not required — 0–3 RIR is the practical optimum.
