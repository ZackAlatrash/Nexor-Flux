# Nutritional Strategies to Improve Sleep Quality and Duration in Athletes

**Full citation:** Doherty R, Madigan S, Warrington G, Ellis J. Sleep and nutrition interactions: Implications for athletes. *Nutrients*. 2019;11(4):822. doi:10.3390/nu11040822  
**PMCID:** PMC6520871  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6520871/  
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
A narrative review examining the bidirectional relationship between sleep and nutrition in athletes, including evidence for specific nutritional interventions that improve sleep quality, sleep onset, and duration — as well as how sleep restriction impacts dietary choices and appetite.

## Key findings & numbers

**Sleep duration recommendations cited:**
- Adolescents: **8–10 h/night**; Adults: **7–9 h/night**; Older adults: **7–8 h/night**
- Sleep < 8 h is "the strongest predictor of injury in adolescent athletes (relative risk = 2.1; 95% CI: 1.2–3.9)"

**Nutritional interventions with sleep benefits:**

| Intervention | Key finding |
|---|---|
| High GI meal 4 h pre-bed | Reduced sleep onset latency from **17.5 ± 6.2 min → 9.0 ± 6.2 min** (p = 0.009) |
| Higher carbohydrate (130 g pre-bed) | Increased REM; decreased light sleep and wakefulness |
| Melatonin (0.3–1 mg) | Effective for circadian timing; "no added benefit to doses >3 mg" |
| Tryptophan-rich protein (α-lactalbumin) | Increased tryptophan:LNAA ratio by **48%**; reduced time awake at night by **19.2–22.1%** |
| Tart cherry juice | +24 min time in bed; +**34 min total sleep duration**; sleep efficiency 82.3% |
| Kiwifruit (2 × 1 h pre-bed, 4 weeks) | +**16.9%** total sleep duration; reduced sleep onset latency by **35.4%** |

**Protein timing (evening):**
- Large protein portions late evening can negatively impact sleep due to thermogenic effect of digestion
- Moderate-sized protein meals (e.g., 40 g casein) are tolerated and support overnight muscle protein synthesis without disrupting sleep

**Sleep restriction and dietary choices:**
- Inadequate sleep increases energy intake (supports findings from Nedeltcheva, Covassin, Spaeth); preference shifts toward high-fat, high-energy foods
- Impaired cognitive control after sleep loss reduces ability to make healthy food choices

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Various nutritional interventions have been shown to improve sleep including high carbohydrate, high glycaemic index evening meals, melatonin, tryptophan rich protein, tart cherry juice, kiwifruit and micronutrients."

> "Sleep duration (< 8 h) has been identified as the strongest predictor of injury in adolescent athletes (RR = 2.1; 95% CI: 1.2–3.9)."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Practical pre-sleep nutrition protocol: moderate carb + tryptophan-containing protein (cottage cheese, turkey, milk) ~2 h before bed
- Tart cherry juice is the single most evidenced whole-food sleep supplement (two studies, meaningful effect sizes)
- Melatonin 0.3–1 mg is appropriate for sleep timing issues; higher doses offer no additional benefit
- High GI carbs 4 h before bed can improve sleep onset — useful for athletes who struggle to fall asleep
- The bidirectional effect matters: poor sleep → worse food choices → worse body composition → cycle must be broken at the sleep end, not just the dietary end
- When a user reports poor sleep, the coach can suggest tart cherry juice or a small carb + protein snack 90 min before bed as low-risk interventions
