# Minimum Effective Dose: 4+ Sets/Week per Muscle; Single Sessions Are Viable

**Full citation:** Iversen VM, Norum M, Schoenfeld BJ, Fimland MS. No Time to Lift? Designing Time-Efficient Training Programs for Strength and Hypertrophy: A Narrative Review. Sports Med. 2021;51(10):2079–2095. doi:10.1007/s40279-021-01490-1. PMC8449772.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449772/
**Access:** open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
Narrative review authored with Schoenfeld, providing evidence-based guidance for time-efficient training to achieve both strength and hypertrophy gains.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Minimum weekly sets:** A minimum of **4 weekly sets per muscle group** induces strength and hypertrophy gains; ≥10 sets provides incrementally better results.
- **Frequency:** Training a muscle **1 day/week** induces similar strength gains as 3+/week if total volume is equated. Frequency matters less than total weekly volume.
- **Load range:** Primary: **6–15 RM** loading for strength and hypertrophy. Alternative: **15–40 repetitions** is acceptable when training to volitional failure.
- **Exercise selection:** Prioritize bilateral multi-joint exercises — at least one leg press/squat variant, one upper-body pull, one upper-body push.
- **Time-saving techniques:** Drop sets, rest-pause, and supersets "roughly halve training time compared to traditional training, while maintaining training volume."
- Single set per exercise performed ≥2×/week produces meaningful strength gains (~20% on average).
- Sessions can be one longer session or multiple shorter sessions — total weekly volume is the key variable.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Performing a minimum of 4 weekly sets per muscle group" for both strength and hypertrophy gains.

> "Training a muscle 1 day per week appears to induce similar strength gains as training ≥3 times per week if the total training volume is the same."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- 4 sets/week is the floor; users struggling to hit 10+ can start at 4 and progress.
- Drop sets and supersets are practical tools for users short on time — worth recommending explicitly.
- 15–40 rep sets work for hypertrophy when going to failure — relevant for users without access to heavy weights.
- One full-body session per week is a legitimate "minimum dose" option for beginners or de-trained users.
- Coach should validate even very low volume efforts ("doing 4 sets is far better than zero") to support adherence.
