# Acute Sleep Loss Reduces Physical Performance by ~7.5% on Average

**Full citation:** Craven J, McCartney D, Desbrow B, Sabapathy S, Bellinger P, Roberts L, Irwin C. Effects of acute sleep loss on physical performance: A systematic and meta-analytical review. *Sports Med*. 2022;52(11):2669-2690. doi:10.1007/s40279-022-01706-y  
**PMCID:** PMC9584849  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9584849/  
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
A systematic review and meta-analysis of 77 studies (n = 959; 89% male; 227 outcome measures) quantifying the effect of acute sleep loss on exercise performance across multiple domains (strength, endurance, anaerobic power, skill, HIIT).

## Key findings & numbers
- **Overall performance decrement:** Mean **−7.56%** (95% CI: −11.9 to −3.13; p = 0.001) across all performance categories
- **Skill tasks:** **−20.9%** (95% CI: −27.0 to −14.9) — most severely impaired domain
- Strength-endurance: −9.85% (95% CI: −19.6 to −0.13)
- Anaerobic power: **−6.26%** (95% CI: −9.10 to −3.41)
- High-intensity interval exercise: **−6.15%** (95% CI: −10.5 to −1.77)
- Endurance: **−5.55%** (95% CI: −8.12 to −2.99)
- Speed/power endurance: −2.90%
- Maximal strength: **−2.85%** (95% CI: −4.47 to −1.23)
- **Lower-body strength:** −3.42% — more affected than upper-body (−1.63%, not significant)
- **Rate of decline:** Performance declined by approximately **0.4% for every additional hour awake** following sleep deprivation or late restriction
- **Time-of-day effect:** Afternoon/evening testing showed consistently larger decrements; morning testing largely unaffected
- Sleep restriction type matters: "Late restriction" (cutting end of sleep) and total deprivation impair performance more than "early restriction" (delayed sleep onset)

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Performance declined by approximately 0.4% for every hour awake following deprivation or late-restriction protocols."

> "Exercise tasks performed in the PM were consistently negatively affected by sleep loss, while tasks performed in the AM were largely unaffected."

> "If sleep loss is anticipated and unavoidable, individuals should avoid situations that lead to experiencing deprivation or late restriction, and prioritise morning exercise in an effort to maintain performance."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- A 7.6% average performance drop from one bad night of sleep is operationally significant for training quality and calorie burn
- Skill-based exercises (technique-heavy movements: Olympic lifts, sport drills) lose ~21% quality — coach should advise against technical skill practice on sleep-deprived days
- Lower-body strength more affected than upper-body — relevant for leg day scheduling around sleep patterns
- "Train in the morning if sleep-deprived" is a practical, actionable coaching tip that reduces risk of poor sessions
- Energy score tracking in the app (1–10) correlates with sleep quality; low energy = likely reduced performance that day
