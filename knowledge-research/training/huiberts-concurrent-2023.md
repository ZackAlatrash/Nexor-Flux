# Concurrent Training: Males Lose More Lower-Body Strength; Females Show No Interference

**Full citation:** Huiberts RO, Wüst RCI, van der Zwaard S. Concurrent Strength and Endurance Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis on the Impact of Sex and Training Status. Sports Med. 2023. doi:10.1007/s40279-023-01943-9. PMC10933151.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933151/
**Access:** open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
Systematic review and meta-analysis examining how sex and training status moderate the interference effect of concurrent (combined endurance + resistance) training on strength, power, and VO₂max adaptations.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Lower-body strength — Males:** Significant interference — ES = −0.43 (95% CI −0.64 to −0.22). Concurrent training compromises lower-body strength gains in men.
- **Lower-body strength — Females:** No significant interference — ES = 0.08 (95% CI −0.34 to 0.49). Women appear resilient to the interference effect (sex difference: p = 0.03).
- **Power adaptations:** Small-to-moderate interference in both sexes: males ES = −0.27; females ES = −0.52. No sex difference in power.
- **Upper-body strength:** No sex-based differences in interference for upper body.
- **VO₂max:** Untrained endurance athletes showed blunted VO₂max improvements with concurrent training (−0.35); trained/highly trained athletes did NOT show this interference.
- Sex and training status are significant moderators of concurrent training outcomes.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Males demonstrated compromised lower-body strength gains with concurrent training (ES −0.43), while females showed no significant interference (0.08)."

> "Inter-individual differences in sex and training status should be considered when optimizing concurrent training prescription."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Male users doing significant cardio volume alongside resistance training should be warned about potential lower-body strength interference.
- Female users appear less susceptible to the concurrent training interference effect for strength — different advice may apply.
- For both sexes, separating resistance and endurance sessions (different days or morning/evening) reduces interference.
- Untrained individuals starting cardio alongside lifting may see slower VO₂max improvements — but this normalizes as fitness improves.
- Running (eccentric component) causes more interference than cycling — mode of cardio matters.
