## Does cardio hurt muscle and strength gains? (concurrent training interference)
tags: cardio, concurrent, running, interference, strength, muscle, gains, hypertrophy, conditioning

Combining cardio and resistance training in the same programme creates a measurable interference effect on lower-body strength — but the impact is sex-dependent. This systematic review and meta-analysis found that **males** showed significant lower-body strength interference with concurrent training (ES = −0.43, meaning roughly 43 % of a standard deviation less strength gain compared to resistance training alone). **Females showed no significant interference** (ES = 0.08, not significant). Power adaptations were blunted in both sexes (males ES −0.27, females ES −0.52). Upper-body strength was unaffected by concurrent training in either sex. The interference effect is real for men doing moderate-volume cardio alongside leg training, but manageable — it is not a reason to avoid cardio entirely.

## How to minimise cardio interference with strength and muscle building
tags: cardio, concurrent, running, interference, strength, muscle, gains, conditioning, program

Practical strategies to reduce concurrent training interference, based on this meta-analysis and the companion network meta-analysis (Chen et al. 2024, 40 RCTs): (1) **Choose HIIT over moderate steady-state** — HIIT running showed the least interference with lower-body strength (SMD = −0.08 vs resistance training alone) compared to moderate-intensity continuous cycling (SMD = −0.38). (2) **Prefer running over cycling** — running includes eccentric contractions that share more neural overlap with resistance training, but HIIT running's shorter duration limits glycogen depletion and fatigue transfer. (3) **Separate sessions** — doing cardio on different days from resistance training, or at least 6–8 hours apart, substantially reduces interference. (4) **Do resistance training first** if both must be done in the same session. For recomposition goals where some cardio is desired, HIIT-based cardio 2–3 × /week alongside 3–4 resistance sessions per week is a well-supported combination.
