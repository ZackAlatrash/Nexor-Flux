# Fueling for Performance — Sports Health Review (2017)

**Full citation:** Bytomski JR. Fueling for Performance. Sports Health. 2017;10(1):47–53. doi: 10.1177/1941738117743913. PMC5753973
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5753973/
**Access:** open access (PMC; SAGE Publications)

## What this source covers
Practical clinical review of macronutrient needs for athletes covering protein, carbohydrate, and fat recommendations, high-protein food sources for omnivores and vegans, injury recovery nutrition, and weight-class sport considerations.

## Key findings & numbers

### Protein Intake Recommendations for Athletes
- Sedentary DRI (general population): 0.8 g/kg body weight/day
- Athlete consensus range (ACSM, ISSN, IOC): 1.2–2.0 g/kg body weight/day
- Fat loss while gaining lean mass: 1.8–2.7 g/kg/day (IOC)
- General caloric contribution: approximately 15–30% of calories from protein
- Injury recovery: 1.2–1.5× usual intake; "20–30 g within 30 minutes" post-exercise during recovery
- Daily distribution: meals and snacks every 3–4 hours

### High-Protein Food Sources Listed
**Animal-based (lean, protein-dense):**
- Lean meats and fish (beef, chicken, turkey, tuna, salmon)
- Cottage cheese
- Eggs
- Plain Greek yogurt
- Whey protein supplements

**Plant-based (for vegan athletes):**
- Lentils
- Tempeh
- Chickpeas
- Black beans
- Quinoa
- Almonds
- Plant-based protein shakes (pea, soy, rice blends)

**High-leucine sources (preferred for MPS):** lean meats, dairy, soy, whey protein

### Carbohydrate Recommendations
- Endurance athletes: 6–10 g/kg/day
- Moderate training: 5–7 g/kg/day
- Intra-exercise (>60 min): 30–60 g/hour
- Post-exercise glycogen: high-GI carbs within 30–60 min of session

### Fat
- Range: 20–35% of total calories
- Omega-3 fatty acids (fish, flaxseed): anti-inflammatory; 1–2 g EPA+DHA/day as supplement if not eating fatty fish

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Recommendations for protein intake in athletes range between 1.2 and 2.0 g per kilogram body weight per day."

> "20–30 g [protein] within 30 minutes [post-exercise]" is recommended for injury recovery.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Evidence strength: MODERATE (practical clinical review; consistent with ISSN standards)
- Provides a clear food-source list organized by dietary pattern (omnivore vs. vegan) — directly usable for app food suggestions
- Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, eggs, and chicken breast are consistently listed as staple high-protein foods
- For plant-based users: tempeh and lentils are highest protein-density plant foods; always pair with complementary protein sources per meal
- Leucine-rich sources (dairy, lean meats, soy, whey) should be the primary protein recommendations for muscle-building contexts
- Injury recovery: temporarily higher protein (up to 2.7 g/kg) plus 20–30 g post-exercise doses
