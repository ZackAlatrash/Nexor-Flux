# Sleep Restriction Shifts Weight Loss Away from Fat and Toward Lean Mass

**Full citation:** Nedeltcheva AV, Kilkus JM, Imperial J, Schoeller DA, Penev PD. Insufficient sleep undermines dietary efforts to reduce adiposity. *Ann Intern Med*. 2010;153(7):435-441. doi:10.7326/0003-4819-153-7-201010050-00006  
**PubMed:** PMID 20921542  
**Source URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20921542/  
**Access:** Abstract-only (author manuscript available via PMC April 2011)

## What this source covers
A randomised crossover trial testing whether sleep curtailment alters the composition of weight lost during caloric restriction in overweight adults, directly addressing the sleep–body-composition relationship during a fat-loss intervention.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Study design:** 10 overweight adults (BMI ~27.4); each crossed over between 5.5 h and 8.5 h sleep/night for 14 days in a closed clinical research environment with **moderate caloric restriction** (−680 kcal/day) for both conditions
- **Total weight loss:** Equivalent in both conditions (~3 kg over 14 days); sleep duration did NOT change how much total weight was lost
- **Fat loss composition shift:**
  - Sleep-restricted (5.5 h): **55% less weight lost as fat** compared to 8.5 h condition
  - Sleep-restricted (5.5 h): **60% more weight lost as fat-free (lean) mass**
- Under sleep restriction, fat oxidation was reduced and resting metabolic adaptations shifted toward catabolism of lean tissue
- **Hunger and ghrelin:** Sleep curtailment elevated hunger ratings and circulating ghrelin levels, increasing appetite during caloric restriction
- Subjects in the 5.5 h condition felt more fatigued and less satisfied despite similar caloric intake

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Sleep restriction decreased the proportion of weight lost as fat by 55% and increased the loss of fat-free mass by 60% compared with the well-rested condition."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- This is the single most important study for recomposition coaching: **inadequate sleep actively undermines fat loss and promotes muscle wasting, even when calories are controlled**
- A user on a deficit who sleeps 5–6 h may be losing significantly more muscle than fat — this should be communicated proactively
- When a user logs consistently low sleep (< 7 h) during a cut, the coach should flag body composition risk and recommend sleep extension
- The hunger/ghrelin finding explains why sleep-deprived users report more cravings — this is physiological, not willpower failure
- Sleep quality and duration should be treated as an equal priority to calorie and protein targets for recomposition goals
