# DIAAS vs. PDCAAS: Protein Quality Scoring (Marinangeli 2017)

**Full citation:** Marinangeli CPF, House JD. Potential impact of the digestible indispensable amino acid score as a measure of protein quality on dietary regulations and health. Nutr Rev. 2017;75(8):658–667. doi:10.1093/nutrit/nux025. PMC5914309.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5914309/
**Access:** Open access

## What this source covers
Review comparing the older PDCAAS protein quality scoring method (current US standard) with the newer FAO-recommended DIAAS method, including practical food scores and implications for dietary recommendations and protein claims.

## Key findings & numbers

### Methodological differences:
- **PDCAAS (Protein Digestibility-Corrected Amino Acid Score):** Uses true fecal nitrogen digestibility for the whole protein; truncates score at 1.0 (cannot exceed 100%). Based on rat bioassay.
- **DIAAS (Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score):** Uses ileal digestibility coefficients for **individual amino acids**; untruncated for single ingredients (can exceed 100); truncated at 100 for mixed meals; FAO-recommended minimum threshold of **75** for protein content claims.

### Food scores (PDCAAS vs. DIAAS):
| Food | PDCAAS | DIAAS | Claim Status under DIAAS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Milk (whole) | 1.00 | 114 | Good source |
| Eggs (hard-boiled) | 1.00 | 113 | Good source |
| Chicken breast | 1.00 | 108 | Excellent source |
| Oatmeal | 0.82 | 84 | Good source |
| Chickpeas | 0.74 | 83 | Good source |
| Tofu | 0.56 | 52 | No claim (below 75 threshold) |

- Marginal differences between PDCAAS and DIAAS for most plant proteins: **1.8–9%** variance.
- Legumes and pulses face significant challenges under DIAAS due to failure to meet the ≥75 threshold.

### Practical protein quality context (from the nutrition science consensus):
- Animal proteins (eggs, dairy, meat, fish): highest biological value, complete amino acid profiles, DIAAS typically >100.
- Soy: complete protein, DIAAS ~91 (competitive with animal sources).
- Pea protein: DIAAS ~82 (good quality, useful in plant-based diets).
- Wheat/rice: low in lysine (limiting amino acid), DIAAS <75 as standalone proteins.
- Protein complementing (rice + beans) can achieve complete amino acid profiles from plant sources.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Foods considered as 'meat alternatives' or 'protein foods' tend to be disadvantaged by the DIAAS system because of failure to meet the DIAAS claim threshold of 75."

> "Marginal [differences], ranging from 1.8% to 9%" between PDCAAS and DIAAS for major plant-based protein sources.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Rank protein sources by quality for users: eggs, dairy, meat, fish > soy > pea > legumes > wheat/rice.
- Users relying solely on plant proteins need more total grams (10–30% more) to compensate for lower DIAAS and to ensure adequate leucine delivery.
- Tofu/soy are acceptable complete plant proteins; avoid framing rice or wheat as equivalent protein sources without combining.
- Protein quality is most relevant when total protein intake is at the lower end of the range; at higher intakes (>2 g/kg/day), quality differences matter less.
