## Does citrulline malate actually work for training performance
tags: citrulline, "citrulline-malate", pump, supplement, "worth-it", evidence, dose, endurance
Evidence strength: LIMITED/MIXED overall. ISSN Category B (limited evidence). L-citrulline for endurance: LOW (meta-analysis shows no significant effect).

**Standard acute dose studied:** 8 g citrulline malate (CM) ~60 minutes before exercise.

**What the evidence shows:**
- **Muscular endurance in resistance training:** the only area with any consistent positive signal. Several studies show modest increases in reps-to-failure in later sets (e.g., bench press set 4–5, leg press) with effect sizes of 0.23–0.59 — described as "subtle." Not all studies replicate this.
- **Aerobic / endurance performance (L-citrulline):** a 2023 meta-analysis of 9 RCTs found NO significant effect on time-to-exhaustion (SMD = 0.03, p = 0.83) or time-to-completion (SMD = −0.07, p = 0.67) in cycling and running. Evidence strength here is LOW.
- **Muscle blood flow and nitric oxide:** 8 g CM did not significantly elevate NO or muscle blood flow in human exercise studies. The proposed "pump" mechanism is not confirmed by performance data.
- **Maximal strength and power:** no benefit documented.

**Quality control issue:** independent testing finds commercial CM products deliver less citrulline than labeled — actual citrulline:malate ratio is often 1.1:1 to 1.6:1 instead of the stated 2:1, meaning an 8 g dose may deliver only ~4.2 g citrulline.

**Safety:** well-tolerated at doses up to 12 g; no adverse blood marker effects.

**Coaching bottom line:** present citrulline as "may modestly help late-set muscular endurance, evidence is mixed." Do not recommend for aerobic performance. The "pump" effect some users feel is real subjectively but does not consistently translate to measurable performance improvement.
