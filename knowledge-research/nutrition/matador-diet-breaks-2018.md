# MATADOR Study: Diet Breaks and Intermittent Energy Restriction (Byrne 2018)

**Full citation:** Byrne NM, Sainsbury A, King NA, Hills AP, Wood RE. Intermittent energy restriction improves weight loss efficiency in obese men: the MATADOR study. Int J Obes. 2018;42(2):129–138. doi:10.1038/ijo.2017.206. PMID: 28925405. PMC5803575.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5803575/
**Access:** Open access

## What this source covers
The MATADOR (Minimising Adaptive Thermogenesis And Deactivating Obesity Rebound) RCT directly compares 16 weeks of continuous energy restriction vs. 8 × 2-week restriction blocks alternating with 7 × 2-week energy balance periods (30 weeks total intervention) in obese men. Primary outcomes: weight loss, fat mass, lean mass, and metabolic adaptation.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Study design:** 47 obese men, parallel-group RCT; both groups received the same total caloric deficit.
  - CON: 16 weeks continuous energy restriction.
  - INT: 8 × 2-week restriction blocks alternating with 7 × 2-week energy balance blocks (30 weeks total).
- **Weight loss (intention-to-treat):** INT: **14.1±5.6 kg** vs. CON: **9.1±2.9 kg** (p<0.001); ~**47–80% greater** weight loss for INT.
- **Fat mass loss:** INT: **12.3±4.8 kg** vs. CON: **8.0±4.2 kg** (p<0.01).
- **Lean mass loss:** Similar between groups (~1.8 kg INT vs. 1.2 kg CON; p=0.4) — diet breaks did not increase lean mass loss.
- Fat comprised **~87±21%** of total weight lost in both groups.
- **Metabolic adaptation (body composition-adjusted REE):** Significantly lower in INT (−360±502 kJ/day) vs. CON (−749±498 kJ/day; p<0.05) — diet breaks reduced metabolic suppression by ~**377 kJ/day (~90 kcal/day)**.
- Mean weight change during the 2-week energy balance blocks: **0.0±0.3 kg** — participants successfully maintained weight during breaks.
- **6-month follow-up:** INT maintained −11.1±7.4 kg vs. CON −3.0±4.4 kg (p=0.001); only INT group maintained >5% weight loss.

## Note on applicability
This study used obese participants (not lean athletes). Diet break protocols for lean/resistance-trained individuals follow the same principle but require different energy balance targets during the break phase. Roberts et al. 2020 discusses this for physique athletes.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Structured diet breaks (2 weeks restriction / 2 weeks maintenance) produce **significantly more fat loss** with less metabolic suppression than continuous restriction.
- Users who have been dieting for 6–8+ weeks are prime candidates to be coached into a 1–2 week maintenance break before resuming.
- Diet breaks do NOT cause more lean mass loss than continuous dieting.
- The mechanism is attenuating adaptive thermogenesis — breaks allow metabolic rate to partially recover.
- This is a strong evidence base for building a "diet break" recommendation into the coaching logic.
