# ISSN Position Stand: Energy Drinks and Energy Shots (2023)

**Full citation:** Jagim AR, Harty PS, Tinsley GM, Kerksick CM, Gonzalez AM, Kreider RB, et al. International society of sports nutrition position stand: energy drinks and energy shots. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2023;20(1):2171314. doi: 10.1080/15502783.2023.2171314
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9987737/
**Access:** open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
ISSN position stand on energy drinks (ED) and energy shots (ES) consumed as beverages for exercise performance. Covers performance outcomes (aerobic, anaerobic, cognitive, team sports), timing, caffeine dose, active ingredients, and special population safety. Excludes powdered pre-workout supplements (those are a separate category).

## Key findings & numbers
- Mean caffeine content in bestselling energy drinks: 174 ± 81 mg per serving
- Effective ergogenic dose: ≥3 mg/kg body weight caffeine (consistent with isolated caffeine position stand)
- FDA adult upper limit: 400 mg/day
- Adolescent recommendation: <400 mg/day maximum; exercise caution; not recommended for children <12
- Timing: consume 10–60 minutes before exercise for optimal acute performance
- Lower-body power: improved with ≥3 mg/kg BW caffeine from ED (Del Coso 2012: 2,726 W vs. 2,549 W placebo half-squat maximal power)
- Endurance performance: moderate-to-large improvements (Ivy 2009: TT 3,690 ± 64 s vs. 3,874 ± 93 s placebo)
- Sprinting: 19.8% and 19.0% sprint time improvement with caffeinated and sugar-free ED (Reis 2021)
- Cognitive function: improved attention and alertness; consistent across studies
- Primary ergogenic ingredients: caffeine and carbohydrate; additive benefits of other ingredients (taurine, B vitamins, guarana) remain undetermined
- Sugar-free formulations: demonstrate similar ergogenic effects to sugar-containing versions for most performance outcomes
- Contraindicated populations: pregnant/breastfeeding women, caffeine-sensitive individuals, cardiovascular/metabolic disease

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Consuming ED and ES 10–60 minutes before exercise can improve mental focus, alertness, anaerobic performance, and/or endurance performance with doses >3 mg/kg BW⁻¹."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Evidence strength: MODERATE-TO-STRONG for caffeine-mediated effects; MIXED for multi-ingredient formulations beyond caffeine
- Key insight: energy drinks work primarily because of their caffeine content; novel ingredients have not been shown to add meaningful performance benefits
- Practical coaching note: if a user uses pre-workout or energy drinks, the dose is best calculated based on caffeine (3–6 mg/kg), not brand marketing claims
- Coach should counsel adolescent users and those with heart conditions against high-dose caffeine sources
- Sugar-containing energy drinks add unnecessary calories; sugar-free is equivalent for performance
