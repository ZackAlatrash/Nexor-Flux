# NASEM / IOM Dietary Reference Intakes: Macronutrients (2005)

**Full citation:** Institute of Medicine. Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy, Carbohydrate, Fiber, Fat, Fatty Acids, Cholesterol, Protein, and Amino Acids. Washington (DC): National Academies Press; 2005. Available from: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK208874/
**Source URL:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK208874/
**Access:** Public domain (U.S. government publication)

## What this source covers
The foundational dietary reference intakes for macronutrients established by the U.S. Institute of Medicine (now NASEM). Sets the RDA, Adequate Intake (AI), and Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Ranges (AMDR) that underpin all U.S. dietary guidance.

## Key findings & numbers

### Protein
- **RDA:** **0.8 g/kg body weight/day** for all healthy adults (men and women).
- **Additional requirements:** Pregnancy: 1.1 g/kg/day; Lactation: 1.1 g/kg/day.
- **AMDR:** **10–35%** of total daily energy intake from protein.
- Note: The 0.8 g/kg RDA was established based on nitrogen balance and represents the minimum to prevent deficiency, not the optimal amount for exercising individuals or body recomposition.

### Carbohydrates
- **RDA:** **130 g/day** for adults and children (minimum needed to supply glucose to the brain).
- **AMDR:** **45–65%** of total daily energy intake from carbohydrates.

### Dietary Fiber
- **Adequate Intake (AI):**
  - Men 19–50 years: **38 g/day**
  - Women 19–50 years: **25 g/day**
  - Men 51+ years: **30 g/day**
  - Women 51+ years: **21 g/day**
  - General rule: **14 g per 1,000 kcal** consumed.
- Note: Average U.S. intake is ~15 g/day — substantially below the AI.

### Fat
- **AMDR:** **20–35%** of total daily energy intake from fat.
- No RDA for total fat; emphasis on fat quality over quantity.
- **Essential fatty acids (Adequate Intake):**
  - Alpha-linolenic acid (omega-3): men **1.6 g/day**, women **1.1 g/day**
  - Linoleic acid (omega-6): men **17 g/day**, women **12 g/day**

### Summary AMDR table
| Macronutrient | AMDR (% of total calories) |
|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | 45–65% |
| Protein | 10–35% |
| Fat | 20–35% |

## Context note on protein RDA vs. exercise recommendations
The 0.8 g/kg/day RDA represents the population minimum to prevent deficiency based on nitrogen balance studies from the 1940s–1970s. The sports nutrition literature (Jäger 2017, Kerksick 2018, Morton 2018) consistently recommends 1.4–2.2+ g/kg/day for individuals engaged in resistance training — approximately 2–3× the RDA. The AMDR of 10–35% is broad enough to accommodate both sedentary and highly active protein needs.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "The recommended dietary allowance for both men and women is set at 0.8 g per kg of body weight." (Ryan-Harshman & Aldoori 2006, summarizing IOM 2005)

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- The population-level RDA (0.8 g/kg) is the regulatory baseline — when users report "meeting the RDA," clarify that exercising individuals need significantly more.
- The AMDR of 10–35% protein allows flexible macro splits; a recomp-focused user eating 2 g/kg protein at 2,000 kcal = ~30% protein, well within range.
- Use the fiber AI (25 g women, 38 g men) as a benchmark target in the app's food tracking.
- Essential fatty acid targets (omega-3: 1.1–1.6 g/day ALA) are relevant for users on very low fat diets.
