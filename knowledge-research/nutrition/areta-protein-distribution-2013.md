# Areta 2013: Protein Distribution and MPS After Resistance Exercise

**Full citation:** Areta JL, Burke LM, Ross ML, et al. Timing and distribution of protein ingestion during prolonged recovery from resistance exercise alters myofibrillar protein synthesis. J Physiol. 2013;591(Pt 9):2319–2331. doi:10.1113/jphysiol.2012.244897. PMID: 23459753. PMC3650697.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3650697/
**Access:** Open access

## What this source covers
A controlled study examining how the timing and size of protein boluses affects myofibrillar protein synthesis (MPS) over 12 hours after a resistance exercise bout. Directly compares pulse, intermediate, and bolus feeding patterns with a fixed total of 80 g whey protein.

## Key findings & numbers
- Total protein: **80 g whey protein isolate** distributed across **12 hours** post-exercise.
- **Three protocols:**
  - PULSE: 8 × 10 g every 1.5 hours
  - INT (intermediate): 4 × **20 g every 3 hours** ← winner
  - BOLUS: 2 × 40 g every 6 hours
- INT produced **31–48% greater MPS** than PULSE and BOLUS combined.
- All protocols increased MPS **88–148% above resting levels** (p < 0.02).
- BOLUS produced the highest early amino acid plasma peaks (**135–205% above rest**) yet inferior MPS.
- PULSE maintained sustained but moderate amino acid elevation — still inferior to INT for MPS.
- Superior protocol: **20 g of rapidly digested high-quality protein every 3 hours**.
- Participants: 24 trained males, mean age 25 years; exercise: leg extension 4×10 at 80% 1RM.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "20 g of whey protein consumed every 3 h was superior to either PULSE or BOLUS feeding patterns for stimulating MPS throughout the day."

> "INT elicited 31–48% greater MPS than PULSE and BOLUS combined."

> "Current dietary guidelines should reconsider protein recommendations as a total daily target without consideration of the pattern of protein intake."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- The optimal post-workout and daily pattern is **20 g every 3 hours**, not frequent small sips nor large infrequent boluses.
- This directly supports recommending **4 protein meals per day** for resistance-training users.
- Flooding amino acid levels (bolus) is not better than well-timed moderate doses.
- Note: this study used whey (fast-digesting) in an isolated 12-hour window; real-world mixed meals with slower proteins may shift the optimal distribution (see Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018).
