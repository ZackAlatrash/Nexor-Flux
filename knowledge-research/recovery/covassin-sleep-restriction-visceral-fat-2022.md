# Sleep Restriction Increases Calorie Intake and Accumulates Visceral Fat Without Changing Expenditure

**Full citation:** Covassin N, Singh P, McCrady-Spitzer SK, et al. Effects of experimental sleep restriction on energy intake, energy expenditure, and visceral obesity. *J Am Coll Cardiol*. 2022;79(13):1254-1265. doi:10.1016/j.jacc.2022.01.038  
**PMCID:** PMC9187217  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9187217/  
**Access:** Open access (NIH manuscript, PMC)

## What this source covers
A randomised, controlled, crossover inpatient study comparing 4-hour vs. 9-hour sleep opportunities over 21 days to quantify effects on energy balance (intake, expenditure, and body composition) in healthy adults under controlled conditions.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Study design:** 12 healthy adults (9 male; mean age 26.5 years); 21-day inpatient protocol; 4 h vs. 9 h sleep opportunity; ad libitum food access throughout
- **Energy intake increase with sleep restriction:** Participants consumed **+308 additional calories/day** (95% CI: 59.2–556.8; p = 0.015) — ~17% more than during acclimation
- Increased intake driven by protein (~13% more) and fat (~17% more), not carbohydrates alone
- **Energy expenditure:** No significant change in BMR, thermic effect of food, or activity-based expenditure (all p > 0.16) — meaning excess intake was fully additive
- **Weight gain:** 0.5 kg greater with sleep restriction (p = 0.008)
- **Total abdominal fat:** +15.2 cm² increase (p = 0.011) during sleep restriction only
- **Visceral fat specifically:** +7.8 cm² (~11% increase) (p = 0.042)
- **Subcutaneous fat:** +7.4 cm² (p = 0.047)
- After return to normal sleep (recovery phase): energy intake normalised within 3 days, but visceral fat continued to accumulate
- Hormonal markers (leptin, ghrelin, cortisol, endocannabinoids) did not significantly change — mechanism may be behavioural/cognitive rather than purely hormonal

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Sleep restriction combined with ad libitum food promotes excess energy intake without varying energy expenditure."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Sleep restriction causes passive caloric overconsumption (~308 kcal/day extra) without metabolic compensation — directly relevant to fat gain risk
- Visceral fat accumulation is particularly concerning for metabolic health during recomposition
- The finding that expenditure doesn't change rules out "sleeping more burns more calories" — the benefit is purely reduced intake and preserved body composition
- Coach should help users understand that poor sleep may be undermining their calorie targets through unconscious overconsumption
- Recovery of intake takes ~3 days after resuming normal sleep; visceral fat takes longer — reinforcing consistency of sleep habits
