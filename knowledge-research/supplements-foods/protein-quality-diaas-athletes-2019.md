# DIAAS in Vegetarian vs. Omnivore Athletes (2019)

**Full citation:** Ciuris C, Lynch HM, Wharton C, Johnston CS. A Comparison of Dietary Protein Digestibility, Based on DIAAS Scoring, in Vegetarian and Non-Vegetarian Athletes. Nutrients. 2019;11(12):3016. doi: 10.3390/nu11123016
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6950041/
**Access:** open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
Comparative study applying DIAAS scoring to actual dietary records of vegetarian vs. omnivorous athletes to determine how dietary protein digestibility differs in practice between eating patterns. Provides real-world DIAAS data by food category.

## Key findings & numbers

### DIAAS Scores by Food Category Used in Study
- Animal flesh (meat, poultry, seafood): 100% digestibility (all amino acids fully available)
- Whey protein concentrate: lysine 91%, SAA 90%, threonine 89%, tryptophan 87%
- Soy protein isolate: lysine 99%, SAA 98%, threonine 98%, tryptophan 95%
- Corn: lysine limited at 66%
- Beans: SAA limited at 77%

### Population-Level Outcomes
- Omnivorous athletes overall DIAAS: 99.9 ± 0.8% (effectively 100% — near-complete digestibility)
- Vegetarian athletes overall DIAAS: 89.9 ± 10.5% (significantly lower; more variability)
- Difference: omnivores 11% higher protein digestibility
- Available protein (g/kg): omnivores = 1.4 g/kg vs. vegetarians = 1.1 g/kg — 43% higher available protein in omnivores
- Total protein consumed (g/kg) was less different; the digestibility gap caused the available protein gap

### Key Practical Finding
- Vegetarian athletes in the study would need "an additional 10 g protein daily on average" to reach recommended intake (1.2 g/kg/day) when DIAAS-adjusted
- This underlines that vegetarian athletes must consume more total protein to achieve equivalent anabolic stimulus

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Based on available protein, as determined through the DIAAS, vegetarian athletes in this study would need to consume, on average, an additional 10 g protein daily to reach the recommended intake for protein (1.2 g/kg/d)."

> "Available protein was 43% higher in the diets of omnivorous athletes versus vegetarian athletes" (1.4 vs. 1.1 g/kg).

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Evidence strength: MODERATE (single study, limited sample size, but consistent with protein quality literature)
- Key coaching application: vegetarian/vegan users need higher gram targets than meat-eaters for equivalent muscle-building stimulus; recommend 1.8–2.4 g/kg/day vs. 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day for omnivores
- Animal flesh (chicken, fish, beef, turkey) has effectively 100% digestibility — these are the most efficient protein sources
- Soy protein isolate has near-animal quality DIAAS (~95–99%); best plant-based protein option
- Corn-based foods (tortillas, corn cereal) are poor protein sources due to lysine limitation
- The 43% "available protein" gap between omnivores and vegetarians highlights the practical importance of source quality, not just gram counts
