# Protein Quality: PDCAAS vs. DIAAS (2022)

**Full citation:** Adhikari S, Schop M, de Boer IJM, Huppertz T. Protein Quality in Perspective: A Review of Protein Quality Metrics and Their Applications. Nutrients. 2022;14(5):947. doi: 10.3390/nu14050947
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8912699/
**Access:** open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
Comprehensive review comparing protein quality scoring methods: PDCAAS (Protein Digestibility-Corrected Amino Acid Score, the older standard) and DIAAS (Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score, the FAO-recommended replacement). Covers methodology, scores for common protein sources, and practical implications for athletes and diet formulation.

## Key findings & numbers

### PDCAAS vs. DIAAS: Methodology Differences
- **PDCAAS**: measures fecal crude protein digestibility (one factor applied to all amino acids); scores truncated at 100%; can overestimate protein quality; older method (FAO 1991)
- **DIAAS**: measures ileal (small-intestinal) digestibility for each individual amino acid separately; untruncated scores (can exceed 100%); preferred methodology because "ileal digesta from humans are most preferred for assessment of protein quality"; FAO adopted 2013

**Critical difference**: Fecal digestibility consistently overestimates ileal digestibility; some products show >10% overestimation difference. DIAAS is more accurate for predicting true bioavailability.

### DIAAS Scores for Common Protein Sources (for population ≥3 years old)
| Protein Source | DIAAS Score | Limiting Amino Acid |
|---|---|---|
| Whey protein concentrate | 133% | Histidine |
| Whey protein isolate | 125% | Histidine |
| Egg | 122% | SAA (Met+Cys) |
| Soy flour | 105% | SAA |
| Soy protein isolate | 98% | SAA |
| Pea protein concentrate | 82% | SAA |
| Rice | 60% | Lysine |

- Animal proteins: generally score >90% DIAAS; classified as "high quality"
- Most plant proteins: score lower; cereals particularly limited by lysine
- Pea protein: moderate (~82%); limited by sulfur amino acids
- Rice protein: poor (~60%); limited by lysine

### Protein Complementarity Timing
- Complementarity works only at the **meal level**, not across the day: "Supply of IAA needs to be synchronous for optimal utilization"
- Athletes cannot offset low-quality breakfast protein with high-quality evening protein
- Pairing lysine-deficient cereals with lysine-rich dairy improves immediate protein utilization

### Processing Effects
- Cooking generally increases digestibility of animal proteins
- Some processing methods can reduce plant protein digestibility
- Heat treatment of whey (e.g., UHT milk) can reduce digestibility vs. pasteurized forms

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "protein quality data are crucial for integration into healthy and sustainable diets, but care is needed in data selection, interpretation"

(From the broader manuscript context:) Use ileal-based (DIAAS) rather than fecal-based (PDCAAS) data when available; combine complementary proteins within single meals, not just across the day.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Evidence strength: STRONG methodological consensus; DIAAS is now the FAO-preferred standard
- Key coaching application: when users ask which protein is "best," whey > egg > casein > soy > pea > rice in DIAAS quality ranking
- Plant-based users: must combine complementary proteins at each meal (e.g., rice + legumes; pea protein + rice protein blend) for equivalent EAA delivery
- For muscle-building: higher DIAAS = more of the protein dose usable for MPS per gram; need 20–40% more plant protein g to match animal protein's EAA delivery
- Practical shorthand: whey protein and eggs are the highest quality; plant proteins need more grams and better combination
