# ISSN Position Stand: Nutrient Timing (Kerksick 2017)

**Full citation:** Kerksick CM, Arent S, Schoenfeld BJ, et al. International society of sports nutrition position stand: nutrient timing. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2017;14:33. doi:10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4. PMC5596471.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5596471/
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
Updated 2017 ISSN position stand on nutrient timing, covering protein and carbohydrate timing for resistance and endurance athletes, the anabolic window concept, pre-sleep nutrition, and practical guidelines.

## Key findings & numbers
- Optimal protein dose per eating occasion: **20–40 g** (0.25–0.40 g/kg body mass) of high-quality protein every 3–4 hours.
- ~**10 g of EAAs** in free form or as part of a 20–40 g protein bolus maximally stimulates MPS.
- Protein timing window post-exercise: "immediately to 2 hours post" ingestion stimulates robust MPS increases.
- Intermediate protein feeding (20 g every 3 h) was superior to either pulse (10 g every 1.5 h) or bolus (40 g every 6 h) feeding for stimulating MPS.
- Pre-sleep protein: **30–40 g casein** 30 min before sleep increases overnight MPS while maintaining overnight lipolysis.
- Daily carbohydrate for high-intensity training: **8–12 g/kg/day** for ≥70% VO2max training upward of 12 h/week.
- Endurance pre-exercise carbohydrate loading: **8–10 g/kg/day** for 1–3 days.
- During exercise (>60 min): **30–60 g carbohydrate/hour** in 6–8% carbohydrate-electrolyte solution every 10–15 min.
- Rapid glycogen restoration (<4 hours): **1.2 g/kg/hour** aggressive refeeding with high glycemic index sources.
- Post-exercise standard: **0.6–1.0 g/kg** within 30 min followed by every 2 hours for 4–6 hours.
- Maximum glycogen restoration within 24 hours requires **≥8 g/kg/day** carbohydrates.
- Adding protein (0.2–0.4 g/kg/h) to carbohydrate increases glycogen resynthesis rate when carbohydrate intake is below 1.2 g/kg/h.
- When total energy is controlled, "meal frequency on weight loss and improving one's body composition is secondary to the total daily caloric intake."

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "20–40 g protein dose (0.25–0.40 g/kg body mass/dose) of a high-quality source every three to 4 h" maximizes muscle protein synthesis.

> "Protein turnover and synthesis rates were greatest when intermediate-sized (20-g) doses of whey protein isolate were consumed every 3 h."

> "The overarching purpose of any nutritional strategy is to enhance the adaptive response to acute and/or chronic exercise."

> "When total energy intake is controlled, and when caloric restriction is employed, the influence of meal frequency on weight loss and improving one's body composition is secondary to the total daily caloric intake."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Protein timing is real but modest in effect; meal timing becomes important only after daily totals are met.
- Recommend protein meals every 3–4 hours, aiming for 20–40 g per sitting.
- Casein or slow-digesting protein source before bed (30–40 g) is a valid and evidence-supported strategy.
- Post-workout carbohydrate matters primarily for endurance athletes with multiple sessions; for recreational lifters, total daily carbs are more important than timing.
- The anabolic window is broader than once thought — 0 to 2 hours post-exercise is practical guidance.
