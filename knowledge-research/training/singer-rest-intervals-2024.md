# Rest Intervals & Hypertrophy: >60s Beats Shorter Rest; Beyond 90s Offers No Extra Benefit

**Full citation:** Singer B, Correa CS, Schoenfeld BJ et al. Give it a rest: a systematic review with Bayesian meta-analysis on the effect of inter-set rest interval duration on muscle hypertrophy. Front Sports Act Living. 2024;6. doi:10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789. PMC11349676.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11349676/
**Access:** open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
Bayesian meta-analysis of 9 RCTs (19 measurements) comparing different inter-set rest interval durations and their effect on muscle hypertrophy. Studies 5–10 weeks; participants primarily 18–35 years.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Binary comparison (≤60s short rest vs >60s longer rest):**
  - Short (≤60s): SMD = 0.48 (95% CrI: 0.19–0.81)
  - Longer (>60s): SMD = 0.56 (95% CrI: 0.24–0.86)
  - Difference favors longer rest, but modest.
- **Four-category analysis:**
  - Short (≤60s): SMD = 0.47
  - Intermediate (61–119s): SMD = 0.65 (peak benefit)
  - Long (120–179s): SMD = 0.55
  - Very long (≥180s): SMD = 0.50
- **Body-region specific:**
  - Arms: SMD +0.13 favoring longer rest (95% CrI −0.27 to 0.51)
  - Thigh: SMD +0.17 favoring longer rest (95% CrI −0.13 to 0.43)
  - Whole body: SMD −0.08 slightly favoring shorter rest
- Differences beyond 90 seconds show no appreciable additional hypertrophic benefit.
- Volume load preservation mediates the advantage of longer rest intervals.
- Contradicts NSCA's previous guideline recommending 30–90s for hypertrophy.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Inter-set rest interval durations >60s" are recommended for hypertrophy; "differences beyond 90 seconds show no appreciable additional benefits."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Default rest interval recommendation: **90–120 seconds** between sets for hypertrophy — the practical sweet spot.
- Very short rest (<60s) is sub-optimal for hypertrophy and should be avoided except for time-efficiency techniques (supersets, circuits).
- Longer rest (3+ minutes) common in powerlifting is fine but not necessary for hypertrophy.
- Intermediate rest (61–119s) appears to produce the best hypertrophic response in this analysis.
