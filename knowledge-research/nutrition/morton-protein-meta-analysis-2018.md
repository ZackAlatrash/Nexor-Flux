# Morton 2018: Protein Supplementation & Resistance Training Meta-Analysis

**Full citation:** Morton RW, Murphy KT, McKellar SR, Schoenfeld BJ, Henselmans M, Helms E, Aragon AA, Devries MC, Banfield L, Krieger JW, Phillips SM. A systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression of the effect of protein supplementation on resistance training-induced gains in muscle mass and strength in healthy adults. Br J Sports Med. 2018;52(6):376–384. doi:10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608. PMID: 28698222. PMC5867436.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5867436/
**Access:** Open access

## What this source covers
The largest systematic review and meta-analysis (49 RCTs, 1,863 participants) examining whether protein supplementation augments resistance training-induced gains in muscle mass and strength, and identifying the dose-response relationship and upper limit.

## Key findings & numbers
- Protein supplementation significantly increased fat-free mass: **+0.30 kg** (95% CI: 0.09–0.52, p=0.007).
- Muscle fiber cross-sectional area increased by **310 µm²** (p=0.02).
- 1-rep max (1RM) strength increased by **2.49 kg** (~9%) beyond training alone (95% CI: 0.64–4.33, p=0.01).
- **Critical dose-response break point: 1.62 g/kg/day** (95% CI: 1.03–2.20). Beyond this intake, protein supplementation produced no further gains in fat-free mass.
- Practical upper limit recommendation: **~2.2 g/kg/day** (upper confidence interval).
- Effects of protein supplementation **decreased with age**: −0.01 kg per year of age (p=0.002).
- Young adults (<45 years): **+0.55 kg FFM**; older adults (>45 years): **+0.06 kg FFM**.
- Resistance-trained individuals gained significantly more: **+0.75 kg FFM** (p=0.03); untrained: **+0.15 kg FFM** (p=0.08, not significant).
- Baseline protein intake across the cohort: ~**1.4 g/kg/day**.
- Study parameters: 49 RCTs, 1,863 participants; mean age 35±20 years; training 6–52 weeks (mean 13±8 weeks).

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Protein supplementation beyond total protein intakes of 1.62 g/kg/day resulted in no further RET-induced gains in FFM."

> "Performance of RET alone is the much more potent stimulus" for strength and muscle gains compared to supplementation alone.

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- **The evidence-based protein ceiling for muscle gain is ~1.62 g/kg/day** (break point), with 2.2 g/kg/day as a practical maximum that accounts for individual variability.
- Note the conflict with ISSN hypocaloric recommendations (2.3–3.1 g/kg/day) — the higher range applies when in a calorie deficit to protect lean mass (not a muscle-building surplus context).
- Protein supplements are beneficial but not magic; resistance training is the dominant stimulus.
- Older users (>45) get less muscle-building benefit from protein supplementation — context-dependent coaching needed.
- This source is the definitive dose-response reference for the "how much protein do I need" question.
