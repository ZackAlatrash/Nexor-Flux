## How much protein per day for exercising adults
tags: protein, macros, muscle, gain, build, how-much, calories, goal
For most exercising individuals, **1.4–2.0 g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day** is sufficient to build and maintain muscle mass. Athletes who are comfortable hitting the lower end (1.4 g/kg) can still make solid progress; pushing toward 2.0 g/kg is a safe ceiling for those in a caloric surplus or at maintenance. Going higher than 2.0 g/kg during a surplus produces little additional muscle-building benefit.

## Protein when cutting — how much to eat in a calorie deficit
tags: protein, cut, cutting, deficit, lose, loss, lean, muscle, macros, how-much
During a calorie deficit, protein needs rise substantially to prevent muscle breakdown. Resistance-trained individuals should target **2.3–3.1 g/kg/day** (based on total bodyweight, or lean body mass for very lean athletes). Higher intakes at the upper end (approaching 3.1 g/kg) also promote additional fat loss via thermic and satiating effects — every gram of protein burned generates 20–35% heat vs. 2–3% for fat.

## Protein per meal — how to distribute protein through the day
tags: protein, meal, meals, frequency, timing, leucine, muscle, gain
Each meal or protein feeding should contain **0.25 g/kg (roughly 20–40 g)** of high-quality protein. Aim for at least **700–3,000 mg of leucine** per dose to reliably trigger muscle protein synthesis. Space meals **every 3–4 hours**. For older adults (>45 years), lean toward 40 g per dose since the leucine threshold rises with age.

## Pre-sleep protein — casein before bed
tags: protein, meal, timing, sleep, bedtime, casein, muscle, gain
Consuming **30–40 g of casein protein** 30–60 minutes before sleep increases overnight muscle protein synthesis and resting metabolic rate without impairing fat burning (lipolysis). Cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, or a casein shake are practical choices. This is one of the few timing windows where protein source choice (slow-digesting casein vs. fast whey) meaningfully changes the outcome.
