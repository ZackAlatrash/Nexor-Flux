# ISSN Position Stand: Caffeine and Exercise Performance (2021)

**Full citation:** Guest NS, VanDusseldorp TA, Nelson MT, Grgic J, et al. International society of sports nutrition position stand: caffeine and exercise performance. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2021;18(1):1. doi: 10.1186/s12970-020-00383-4. PMID: 33388079
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7777221/
**Access:** open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
Comprehensive ISSN position stand on caffeine supplementation and exercise performance, covering dosing, timing, exercise-type specificity, caffeine forms, individual variability, special populations, and safety. Published January 2, 2021.

## Key findings & numbers
- Effective dose: 3–6 mg/kg body mass; minimal effective dose may be as low as 2 mg/kg
- Very high doses (≥9 mg/kg) associated with high incidence of side effects and provide no additional ergogenic benefit
- Timing: typically 60 minutes pre-exercise; alternative forms (chewing gum, mouth rinse) may act faster
- Aerobic endurance: most consistent moderate-to-large benefits; consistently improves time-trial and time-to-exhaustion performance
- Other benefits with small-to-moderate effect sizes: muscular endurance, movement velocity, muscular strength, sprinting, jumping, throwing
- Caffeine forms studied: anhydrous capsules, caffeinated gum, mouth rinses, energy gels, chews, coffee
- Half-life: approximately 5–6 hours; last dose ≥8 hours before bedtime recommended to protect sleep
- Tolerance/habituation: regular caffeine users may experience attenuated ergogenic effects; abstaining 7 days prior may restore sensitivity
- Habitual caffeine intake does not fully eliminate ergogenic effect but may reduce magnitude
- Individual variation: genetic polymorphisms in CYP1A2 and ADORA2A influence response; some individuals are "non-responders"
- FDA recommended upper limit: 400 mg/day for healthy adults
- Adolescents: exercise caution; not recommended in high doses

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Caffeine has consistently been shown to improve exercise performance when consumed in doses of 3–6 mg/kg body mass with minimal effective doses of caffeine currently remaining unclear, but they may be as low as 2 mg/kg body mass."

> "Aerobic endurance appears to be the form of exercise with the most consistent moderate-to-large benefits from caffeine use, although the magnitude of its effects differs between individuals."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Evidence strength: STRONG (one of the best-evidenced ergogenic aids in sports nutrition)
- Practical dose for coaching: 3–6 mg/kg ~60 min pre-workout; for a 80 kg user ≈ 240–480 mg
- Warn users about sleep disruption if taken in the afternoon; advise cutoff at ≥8 hours before bed
- Note that pre-workout supplements and energy drinks are the most common delivery format; the active ingredient is caffeine itself
- Tolerance breaks are optional but may enhance effect; cycling off for 1 week restores sensitivity
