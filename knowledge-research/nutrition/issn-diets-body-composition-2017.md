# ISSN Position Stand: Diets and Body Composition (Aragon 2017)

**Full citation:** Aragon AA, Schoenfeld BJ, Wildman R, et al. International society of sports nutrition position stand: diets and body composition. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2017;14:16. doi:10.1186/s12970-017-0174-y. PMID: 28630601. PMC5470183.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5470183/
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
A comprehensive ISSN position stand comparing the effects of different diet types (low-fat, low-carbohydrate, ketogenic, high-protein, intermittent fasting) on body composition. Covers energy balance principles, macronutrient roles, and compliance factors.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Low-fat diets (LFD):** 20–35% of calories from fat; reducing fat modestly but consistently reduces body weight and fat mass.
- **Low-carbohydrate diets (LCD):** Very-LCD (≤10% CHO) showed slightly greater fat mass reduction than mild-LCD (~40% CHO); mean differences small (0.57–1.46 kg).
- **Ketogenic diets (KD):** ~50 g CHO maximum; 60–80%+ energy from fat; protein 1.2–1.5 g/kg. All controlled studies matching protein and energy between KD and non-KD **failed to show a fat loss advantage** for keto.
- KD produced spontaneous energy intake reductions of **~294 kcal/day** under ad libitum conditions (appetite suppression).
- KD may impair high-intensity performance due to decreased pyruvate dehydrogenase activity.
- **High-protein diets (HPD):** 2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM under hypocaloric conditions for lean, resistance-trained athletes; 1.4–2.0 g/kg BW standard athletic range.
- Protein thermic effect: **20–35%** of consumed calories; carbohydrate: 5–15%; fat: 2–3%.
- TEF represents **8–15%** of total daily energy expenditure.
- **Intermittent fasting (IF):** "No significant advantage over daily caloric restriction for improving body composition."
- Fat loss rate: **0.5–1.0% body weight per week** recommended to preserve lean mass in leaner subjects.
- Muscle gain: sustained caloric surplus required; larger surpluses (544–2,010 kcal/day) appropriate for untrained beginners; smaller for advanced trainees.
- **Adaptive thermogenesis (AT):** 10–15% TDEE reduction beyond predicted loss from tissue changes with ≥10% body weight loss.
- Mitigation of AT: higher protein + resistance training circumvents metabolic slowdown seen with low-protein VLEDs.
- TDEE components: BMR 60–70%, NEAT 15–50%, Exercise Activity 15–30%, TEF 8–15%.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "A wide range of dietary approaches (low-fat to low-carbohydrate/ketogenic, and all points between) can be similarly effective for improving body composition."

> "All controlled interventions to date that matched protein and energy intake between KD and non-KD conditions have failed to show a fat loss advantage."

> "The long-term success of a diet depends upon compliance and suppression or circumvention of mitigating factors such as adaptive thermogenesis."

> "Diets primarily focused on accruing LM are driven by a sustained caloric surplus to facilitate anabolic processes and support increasing resistance-training demands."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Diet type matters less than total calories + protein; coach users toward whichever macro split they can adhere to.
- When in a calorie deficit, raise protein to 2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM; this is the most evidence-backed lever.
- Fat loss rate of 0.5–1%/week body weight protects lean mass; use this to set realistic expectations.
- Ketogenic diets are not superior for fat loss when calories and protein are matched; useful mainly for hunger management in some individuals.
- Adaptive thermogenesis is real — users who plateau should not just cut further; diet breaks, resistance training, and adequate protein are the evidence-based mitigations.
