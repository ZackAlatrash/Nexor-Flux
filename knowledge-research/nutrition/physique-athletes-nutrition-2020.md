# Nutritional Recommendations for Physique Athletes (Roberts et al. 2020)

**Full citation:** Roberts BM, Helms ER, Trexler ET, Fitschen PJ. Nutritional Recommendations for Physique Athletes. J Hum Kinet. 2020;71:79–108. doi:10.2478/hukin-2019-0096. PMID: 32148575. PMC7052702.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7052702/
**Access:** Open access

## What this source covers
Comprehensive guide for male and female physique athletes covering contest prep and offseason nutrition, macro targets, weight loss rates, peak week strategies, hormonal considerations, psychological risks, and supplement use.

## Key findings & numbers

### Protein
- Recommended range: **1.8–2.7 g/kg/day**, or up to **3.5 g/kg/day** for hunger control during aggressive cuts.
- Meta-analytic upper CI for FFM maintenance: **2.2 g/kg/day**; higher protein provides satiety and mood benefits.
- Higher protein (2.4 g/kg/day) vs. lower (1.2 g/kg/day) during **40% caloric deficit** resulted in more lean mass preservation AND greater fat loss.
- Protein distribution: **4–5 protein boluses daily**; space meals with one within 1–2 hours of training; one pre-sleep.

### Carbohydrate
- Recommended: **2–5 g/kg/day** during contest prep.
- Peak week carbohydrate loading used by **83%** of competitors.
- Each gram of glycogen stores **minimum 3–4 g of water** — relevant for muscle fullness.
- Potential for glycogen supercompensation: storing **up to 1.79×** initial glycogen after 3-day depletion.

### Fat
- **10–25% of total calories** during contest prep (broadened from prior 15–20% recommendation).
- Authors strongly caution against very low fat intake for extended periods.
- Testosterone relationship is tied to **energy availability** more than fat percentage per se.

### Caloric Deficit & Weight Loss Rate
- Recommended rate: **≤0.5% of body mass per week** to minimize lean mass loss.
- Slower rate (0.46%/week) linked to higher competition placements vs. non-placers (>0.5%/week).
- 0.7% and 1.0% weekly rates produced higher lean mass losses in males.
- Caloric deficit reduces muscle protein synthesis by **~27%** after 5 days, **19%** after 10 days in lean individuals.

### Offseason Calories
- Males: ~**3,800 kcal/day**; females: ~**2,000 kcal/day**
- Competition phase: both sexes as low as ~**1,600 kcal/day**

### NEAT (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis)
- Individual NEAT variation: up to **2,000 kcal/day** between similar-sized individuals.
- 10% body weight reduction = ~**262 kcal/day** NEAT reduction; 20% reduction = ~**500 kcal/day** decrease.

### Peak Week
- Recommended: do **not** reduce sodium, water, or carbohydrates aggressively.
- Sodium elimination may negatively impact muscle pump and glycogen storage.

### Post-Competition Recovery
- 4–6 months for full physiological recovery.
- Early weight regain is preferential fat gain.
- Leptin and testosterone may require 5–6 months to normalize.

### Supplements (prevalence in competitive physique athletes)
- Protein powder: 75–88.9% use; creatine: 39–49.4% males; caffeine: 322 mg/day males, 252 mg/day females.
- **Creatine:** 20 g/day loading (0.3 g/kg) × ~1 week, then 5 g/day maintenance. Causes 1–2 kg water weight gain.
- **Caffeine:** 3–6 mg/kg ~1 hour before exercise; enhances maximal strength, power, muscular endurance.
- **Citrulline malate:** 8 g ~1 hour before exercise; small but significant effect on repetitions to fatigue.

### Psychological risks
- 81.5% of male bodybuilders preoccupied with food; 46% reported binge eating post-competition.
- 42% of female competitors historically anorexic; 67% terrified of becoming fat.
- Female athletes recommended **≥30 kcal/kg lean mass** daily to prevent amenorrhea; 63% of female competitors reported menstrual abnormalities during prep.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Slower rates of weight loss (≤0.5% of body mass per week) are generally preferable for attenuating unfavorable adaptations."

> "Higher protein meals increase acute ratings of fullness."

> "Each gram of glycogen can result in a minimum of 3-4 grams of stored water."

> "The early phase of weight regain is characterized by preferential gain of fat mass."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- For users in a cut: target 1.8–2.7 g/kg protein, ≤0.5%/week weight loss, fat ≥10% of calories, carbs at 2–5 g/kg.
- NEAT suppression during dieting is real and significant — flag for users who stall despite adherence.
- Female users need special attention to energy availability (≥30 kcal/kg LBM) and hormonal health.
- Carb loading before events/check-ins is valid and widely practiced; 3–4 g water per g glycogen is the mechanism.
