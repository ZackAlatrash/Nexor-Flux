## Optimal training prescription for muscle strength (sets, frequency, load)
tags: strength, muscle, sets, frequency, load, intensity, "1rm", reps, "rep-range", "how-many", "how-often"

For **muscle strength**, the top-ranked prescription in this Bayesian network meta-analysis of 178 studies (5,097 participants, 45 % women) was higher-load, multi-set training performed **3 times per week per muscle group** — SMD 1.60 (95 % CI 1.38–1.82). There was a 95 % probability that this combination caused the largest strength gains of any prescription tested. The minimum viable dose for near-certain strength improvement was **at least 2 sets or 2 sessions per week** (95 % probability of meaningful gain). Importantly, every resistance training prescription tested — even low-volume, low-frequency — was superior to no training at all, making the first priority simply starting and being consistent.

## Optimal training prescription for hypertrophy (sets, frequency, load)
tags: hypertrophy, muscle, sets, frequency, load, intensity, "1rm", reps, "rep-range", gains, "how-many", "how-often"

For **muscle hypertrophy**, the top-ranked prescription in this analysis of 119 studies (3,364 participants, 47 % women) was higher-load, multi-set training done **2 times per week per muscle group** — SMD 0.66 (95 % CI 0.47–0.85), with an 86.9 % probability of ranking in the top three. Importantly, **load was less influential than volume for hypertrophy** — meaning that accumulating enough total sets matters more than whether those sets are heavy or moderate. Training 3 × /week also worked well for hypertrophy; the 2 × /week edge in this ranking likely reflects the most commonly studied protocol rather than a hard rule. The practical prescription: 2–3 sessions per muscle per week, multiple sets per session (3–5), with loads in the 60–85 % 1RM range (6–20 rep range), taken to 1–3 reps in reserve.
