## Diet breaks produce more fat loss than continuous dieting
tags: diet-break, plateau, fat, loss, lose, cut, cutting, adherence, calories, calorie, refeed, weight
The MATADOR RCT (47 men, same total caloric deficit) compared 16 weeks of continuous restriction versus alternating 2-week restriction / 2-week maintenance blocks over 30 weeks. The intermittent group lost **14.1 kg vs. 9.1 kg** — roughly 47–80% more fat loss — with no additional lean mass loss (both groups lost ~1.5 kg lean). At 6-month follow-up, only the diet-break group maintained >5% weight loss (−11.1 kg vs. −3.0 kg). The mechanism: diet breaks reduced metabolic suppression by ~90 kcal/day, allowing resting energy expenditure to partially recover before the next restriction phase.

## Metabolic adaptation is real and persistent
tags: plateau, metabolic-adaptation, tdee, calories, calorie, weight, fat, loss, deficit, diet-break
The Biggest Loser study (14 contestants, 6-year follow-up) quantified how persistently dieting slows metabolism. After the competition, resting metabolic rate was **610 kcal/day below baseline**; six years later it was still **704 kcal/day below baseline** — the adaptation worsened over time rather than recovering. The body composition-adjusted component (true metabolic slowing beyond tissue loss) reached **−499 kcal/day** at the 6-year mark. This study used extreme rapid weight loss which amplifies adaptation, but the persistence finding matters for all dieters. Practical takeaway: calorie targets set at the start of a diet will progressively over-estimate TDEE; recalibrate every 4–8 weeks of active dieting.

## When to recommend a diet break
tags: diet-break, refeed, plateau, adherence, fat, loss, calories, calorie, weight, tdee, metabolic-adaptation
A structured diet break — eating at maintenance for **2 weeks** after every 6–8 weeks of restriction — is supported by MATADOR evidence. Key points: (1) participants maintained weight during breaks (0.0±0.3 kg change); (2) lean mass was not lost during breaks; (3) breaks attenuated adaptive thermogenesis, making subsequent restriction phases more effective. Practical coaching signal: recommend a diet break when a user has been in a deficit for 6–8+ weeks, weight loss has stalled for 2–3 weeks despite reported adherence, or subjective fatigue and hunger are very high. Breaking does not undo progress.
