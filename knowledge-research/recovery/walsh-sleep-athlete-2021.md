# Walsh 2021 IOC/BJSM Consensus: Sleep and the Athlete

**Full citation:** Walsh NP, Halson SL, Sargent C, Roach GD, Nédélec M, Gupta L, et al. Sleep and the athlete: narrative review and 2021 expert consensus recommendations. *Br J Sports Med*. 2021;55(7):356-368. doi:10.1136/bjsports-2020-102025  
**PubMed:** PMID 33144349  
**Source URL:** https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/16297/1/Walsh%202020_%20Sleep%20and%20the%20Athlete%202021_%20Consensus%20Review_%20BJSM_%20With%20Supplementary%20Material.pdf  
**Access:** Open access (BJSM; institutional repository)

## What this source covers
Landmark IOC-aligned narrative review and expert consensus synthesising evidence on sleep in athletes, covering sleep needs, consequences of poor sleep, monitoring approaches, and a practical "sleep toolbox" of evidence-ranked interventions.

## Key findings & numbers
- General adult recommendation is 7–9 hours/night; authors note this may be insufficient for athletes who may need closer to **8–10 hours**
- Athletes habitually sleep less than the recommended amounts; poor sleep is prevalent across elite sports
- "Habitual short sleep" defined as < 7 hours/night — associated with impaired recovery, immune function, and injury risk
- Athletic performance is reduced by **a night or more without sleep**; impact of 1–3 nights of partial sleep restriction on performance is less clear but still concerning
- Sleep toolbox interventions ranked: sleep extension and strategic napping are the most evidenced; sleep hygiene counselling (consistent schedule, dark/cool room, no screens) has a foundational role
- Napping (20–90 min) can partly compensate for prior sleep debt and restore performance
- Research quality is mixed: many studies use inconsistent methods, limiting firm effect size estimates
- A standardised "sleep screening tool" is recommended for athletic settings to identify at-risk individuals
- Light manipulation (morning light exposure, evening blue-light blocking) has supporting evidence for circadian alignment

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Athletic performance is reduced by a night or more without sleep."

> "The quality of much of the available research evidence is poor, largely due to inconsistent, unreliable and invalid research methods."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- The consensus validates the coach recommending 8–10 h nightly for active athletes — higher than general population guidance
- Sleep screening (asking about sleep duration, quality, timing) should be standard — the Hooper Index or Epworth scale can be built in
- Sleep extension and napping are the most actionable first-line interventions; complex tech gadgets rank lower
- Poor sleep is a strong signal to reduce training intensity that day — directly feeds readiness/deload logic
