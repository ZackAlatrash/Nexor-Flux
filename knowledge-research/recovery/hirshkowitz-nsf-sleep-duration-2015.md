# National Sleep Foundation Sleep Duration Recommendations

**Full citation:** Hirshkowitz M, Whiton K, Albert SM, Alessi C, Bruni O, DonCarlos L, et al. National Sleep Foundation's sleep time duration recommendations: methodology and results summary. *Sleep Health*. 2015;1(1):40-43. doi:10.1016/j.sleh.2014.12.010  
**PubMed:** PMID 29073412  
**Source URL:** https://www.sleephealthjournal.org/article/S2352-7218(15)00015-7/fulltext  
**Access:** Open access (abstract + summary; full text via Sleep Health journal)

## What this source covers
The National Sleep Foundation convened an 18-member expert panel from 12 medical and scientific organisations to review and update recommended sleep durations across the entire lifespan, using a formal consensus and voting process.

## Key findings & numbers
Recommended daily sleep duration ranges by age group:

| Age group | Recommended range | May be appropriate |
|---|---|---|
| Newborns (0–3 months) | 14–17 h | 11–13 h / 18–19 h |
| Infants (4–11 months) | 12–15 h | 10–11 h / 16–18 h |
| Toddlers (1–2 years) | 11–14 h | 9–10 h / 15–16 h |
| Preschoolers (3–5 years) | 10–13 h | 8–9 h / 14 h |
| School-age (6–13 years) | 9–11 h | 7–8 h / 12 h |
| Teenagers (14–17 years) | **8–10 h** | 7 h / 11 h |
| Young adults (18–25 years) | **7–9 h** | 6 h / 10–11 h |
| Adults (26–64 years) | **7–9 h** | 6 h / 10 h |
| Older adults (≥65 years) | **7–8 h** | 5–6 h / 9 h |

- Durations < 6 hours or > 10–11 hours in adults are "not recommended"
- The panel used a modified RAND/UCLA Appropriateness Method with literature review across 312 articles
- Final recommendations reflect scientific consensus, not just the majority of the evidence

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Sleeping less than the recommended amount is associated with attention, behavior and learning problems. Insufficient sleep also increases the risk of accidents, injuries, hypertension, obesity, diabetes, and depression."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- **7–9 h is the evidence-based adult target** — the coach should reference this when users ask "how much sleep do I need?"
- Athletes likely need the upper end (8–9 h) or beyond, per sport-specific consensus (Walsh et al.)
- Teenagers in the app (14–17) need 8–10 h — relevant for adolescent athletes
- Sleeping < 6 h is clearly in the "not recommended" zone; the coach should flag this as a recovery risk factor
- Sleep duration is a laggable metric: pairing it with soreness, energy, and training quality scores reveals patterns
