# Dose-Response Meta-Regressions: Both Volume & Frequency Show Diminishing Returns

**Full citation:** Pelland J, Remmert J, Robinson Z, Hinson S, Zourdos MC. The Resistance Training Dose-Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gain. SportRxiv. 2024. doi:10.51224/SRXIV.460.
**Source URL:** https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460
**Access:** open access (SportRxiv preprint; also published in J Strength Cond Res PMID 41343037)

## What this source covers
Meta-regression analysis of 67 studies (2,058 participants) examining how weekly volume and frequency relate to hypertrophy and strength gains, with a novel direct/indirect set classification approach.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Volume effects:** Gains in muscle size AND strength increase as weekly volume increases — but both show **diminishing returns**. Strength shows considerably more pronounced diminishing returns than hypertrophy.
- **Frequency effects:** For hypertrophy, frequency effects are mixed (posterior probability of marginal slope > 0 is less than 100%). For strength, gains consistently increased with frequency but with diminishing returns.
- **Novel methodology:** Distinguishes "direct sets" (exercises targeting the muscle directly) from "indirect sets" (where the muscle is a secondary mover). Uses fractional quantification: direct = 1.0, partial involvement = 0.5, minimal = 0.
- **Key insight:** "Distinguishing between direct and indirect sets appears essential for predicting adaptations to a given resistance training protocol." Counting all exercises equally overestimates effective volume.
- 67 total studies; 2,058 participants.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Gains in muscle size and strength increase as volume increases" with "both outcomes displaying diminishing returns."

> "Distinguishing between direct and indirect sets appears essential for predicting adaptations to a given resistance training protocol."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Volume tracking should distinguish exercises that directly train a muscle vs those with secondary involvement.
- E.g., bench press = direct chest/triceps sets; overhead press = indirect chest, direct shoulder/triceps.
- At very high volumes (>20 sets/week), each additional set contributes less — this is the diminishing-returns zone.
- Practical implication: 10–20 direct sets/week is where most of the hypertrophy gains occur; beyond 20 is hard to justify for most natural athletes.
- Frequency increases should be viewed as a way to distribute volume more manageably, not as a primary driver.
