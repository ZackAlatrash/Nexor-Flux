# Sleep Extension Improves Athletic Performance in Collegiate Basketball Players

**Full citation:** Mah CD, Mah KE, Kezirian EJ, Dement WC. The effects of sleep extension on the athletic performance of collegiate basketball players. *Sleep*. 2011;34(7):943-950. doi:10.5665/SLEEP.1132  
**PMCID:** PMC3119836  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3119836/  
**Access:** Open access (PMC)

## What this source covers
A prospective intervention study examining whether extending nightly sleep duration above habitual levels improves sport-specific athletic performance in elite collegiate basketball players across a full competitive season.

## Key findings & numbers
- N = 11 healthy male Stanford varsity basketball players; mean age 19.4 ± 1.4 years
- Baseline sleep: ~2–4 weeks at habitual schedule; extension phase: 5–7 weeks targeting ≥10 hours in bed per night
- Objective sleep increased by **110.9 ± 79.7 minutes** (p < 0.001): from 400.7 ± 61.8 min to 507.6 ± 78.6 min per night
- Sprint time (282-foot shuttle): **16.2 sec → 15.5 sec** (p < 0.001) — a ~4% improvement
- Free throw accuracy: **7.9 → 8.8 of 10 shots (9% improvement)** (p < 0.001)
- 3-point field goal accuracy: **10.2 → 11.6 of 15 shots (9.2% improvement)** (p < 0.001)
- Mean PVT (psychomotor vigilance task) reaction time decreased significantly (p < 0.01)
- Daytime sleepiness (Epworth Sleepiness Scale): **9.64 → 3.36** (p < 0.001) — large reduction
- POMS vigor increased; fatigue decreased (p < 0.001)
- Study was conducted during an actual competitive season, strengthening ecological validity

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Optimal sleep is likely beneficial in reaching peak athletic performance."

> "Subjects showed significantly faster sprint times, increased shooting accuracy on free throws and 3-point field goals."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Even well-sleeping athletes likely carry a sleep debt; targeting 8–10 h is an evidence-based, zero-cost performance lever
- Mood and energy scores (vigor ↑, fatigue ↓) from extended sleep directly map to app wellness tracking fields
- The 9% shooting accuracy gains after ~6 weeks of extra sleep illustrate compounding recovery benefit over a training block
- Coach should recommend sleep extension (add 30–60 min/night) as first-line intervention when an athlete reports soreness, fatigue, or poor training quality
