# Overtraining Syndrome: Definitions, Prevalence, and the Need for Multidisciplinary Recovery

**Full citation:** Armstrong LE, Bergeron MF, Lee EC, Mershon JE, Armstrong EM. Overtraining syndrome as a complex systems phenomenon. *Front Netw Physiol*. 2022;1:794392. doi:10.3389/fnetp.2021.794392  
**PMCID:** PMC10013019  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10013019/  
**Access:** Open access (CC BY 4.0)

## What this source covers
A conceptual review proposing that overtraining syndrome (OTS) is a complex systems phenomenon involving genomic, epigenetic, hormonal, neural, and psychosocial interactions, synthesising current understanding of definitions, prevalence, diagnosis, and treatment.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Functional overreaching (FOR):** Short-term performance deterioration following intensified training; resolves within **days to weeks** with rest/recovery; associated with desired physiological adaptations
- **Nonfunctional overreaching (NFOR):** Prolonged intensified training producing maladaptations; requires **months** of recovery; performance does NOT fully recover between training blocks
- **Overtraining syndrome (OTS):** Defined as accumulation of training AND/OR non-training stress resulting in long-term performance decrements requiring **several weeks to months** to resolve
- **Prevalence:** Research shows **64% of female and 66% of male** elite distance runners reported experiencing staleness at some point during their careers
- **Symptoms of OTS:** Generalised fatigue, insomnia, change of appetite, irritability, loss of motivation, lack of concentration, frequent infections
- **Multi-factorial risk factors:** Insufficient caloric intake, low protein/carbohydrate intake, poor sleep quality, excessive cognitive effort, and psychological stress all compound the risk
- **No single diagnostic biomarker** definitively identifies OTS — it remains a diagnosis of exclusion
- **Treatment:** "Rest with optimal nutrition" is the established primary treatment; recovery timeline is indefinite and unpredictable
- **Recommended support team:** Physician, sports dietitian, and sports psychologist working in concert

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "64% of female and 66% of male elite distance runners reported experiencing staleness at some point during their competitive careers."

> "The established and most successful treatment is rest with optimal nutrition, allowing the body to recover with the hope that performance competencies can be regained."

> "OTS results from multiple risk factors and behaviors, including insufficient caloric, protein and carbohydrate intake, poor sleep quality, and excessive cognitive effort outside of the training environment."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- **Deload weeks** directly address functional overreaching — systematic volume reduction prevents progression to NFOR or OTS
- Low energy availability (aggressive caloric deficit) combined with high training volume is a primary OTS risk factor — recomposition users are particularly vulnerable
- Sleep quality appears explicitly as an OTS risk factor — reinforcing the sleep-recovery-training trifecta
- Tracking soreness (1–10), energy (1–10), and training quality scores over time allows early detection of overreaching patterns before they progress to OTS
- If a user shows 2+ weeks of declining energy scores + soreness + performance regression, the coach should proactively recommend a structured deload
- The 64–66% career prevalence in elite runners suggests even serious recreational athletes face meaningful overtraining risk
