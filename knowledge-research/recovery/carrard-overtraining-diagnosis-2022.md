# Diagnosing Overtraining Syndrome: Scoping Review of 39 Publications

**Full citation:** Carrard J, Rigort AC, Appenzeller-Herzog C, Colledge F, Königstein K, Hinrichs T, Schmidt-Trucksäss A. Diagnosing overtraining syndrome: A scoping review. *Sports Health*. 2022;14(5):665-673. doi:10.1177/19417381211044739  
**PMCID:** PMC9460078  
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9460078/  
**Access:** Open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0)

## What this source covers
A scoping review of 39 publications (952 subjects; 328 with OTS, 624 controls) examining available diagnostic tools and biomarkers for overtraining syndrome (OTS), nonfunctional overreaching (NFOR), and functional overreaching (FOR).

## Key findings & numbers
**Taxonomy of performance decrements:**
- **Functional overreaching:** Performance decrement up to ~2 weeks → supercompensation (desirable adaptation)
- **Nonfunctional overreaching:** Performance decrement lasting **3–4 weeks** without supercompensation
- **Overtraining syndrome:** Reduced performance lasting **> 3–4 weeks** with neither supercompensation nor improved performance

**Hormonal markers (most common findings):**
- Diminished testosterone/estradiol ratio (found in 4 studies)
- Elevated cortisol levels (4 studies)
- Elevated nocturnal catecholamines (3 studies)
- Blunted GH (growth hormone), ACTH, and prolactin responses to stimulation

**Psychological markers:**
- Profile of Mood States (POMS): increased fatigue, tension, depression; decreased vigor
- These subjective wellbeing markers are among the most consistently reported

**Other biomarkers identified:**
- HRV changes (parasympathetic dominance early; potential HRV decrease later)
- Elevated inflammatory markers: IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α
- Diminished glutamine/elevated glutamate ratio
- Altered redox homeostasis
- EEG pattern changes

**Diagnostic systems reviewed:**
- EROS-CLINICAL (clinical items only)
- EROS-SIMPLIFIED (clinical + biochemical)
- EROS-COMPLETE (clinical, biochemical, insulin tolerance test, body composition)

**Prevalence note:** Around **30% of young English athletes (age 15)** across multiple sports showed OTS characteristics.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Overtraining Syndrome: Reduced performance capacity lasts for more than 3 to 4 weeks and is followed by neither supercompensation nor improved athletic performance."

> "Functional Overreaching: A short-term decrease in performance (usually up to 2 weeks) followed by a supercompensation effect."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- The 2-week threshold: if a user's strength/performance has declined for > 2 weeks despite training, this is a red flag for NFOR or OTS
- POMS-based wellbeing tracking (mood, fatigue, vigor) is the most practically accessible diagnostic tool — directly maps to the app's daily wellness check-in
- HRV + subjective wellbeing scores together provide a more robust picture than either alone
- The testosterone/cortisol balance links to body composition: chronically elevated cortisol in OTS promotes muscle catabolism and fat retention
- 30% adolescent prevalence underscores that OTS is not a rare elite-athlete problem — it affects recreational athletes too
