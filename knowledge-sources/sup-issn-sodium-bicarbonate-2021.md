## Does baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) improve performance and how much to take
tags: "sodium-bicarbonate", "baking-soda", bicarb, buffering, dose, "how-much", supplement, "worth-it"
Evidence strength: STRONG for high-intensity efforts lasting 30 seconds to 12 minutes (ISSN Category A / AIS Group A). Limited for longer efforts.

**Mechanism:** sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃) is an extracellular pH buffer. It attenuates accumulation of lactate and hydrogen ions (H⁺) during glycolytic metabolism, delaying fatigue in high-intensity efforts.

**Single-dose protocol:**
- Dose: **0.3 g/kg body weight** (optimal). Minimum effective dose is 0.2 g/kg.
- Timing: **60–180 minutes before exercise**. Peak blood buffering occurs ~60–90 minutes post-ingestion.
- Example: 80 kg athlete → 24 g (~1.5 tablespoons or ~6 × 500 mg capsules).

**Multi-day loading (reduces GI side effects):** 0.4–0.5 g/kg/day for 3–7 consecutive days; achieves equivalent blood buffering with less acute GI distress.

**Best exercise types (documented benefit):** boxing, judo, wrestling, karate, taekwondo; high-intensity cycling; 200–400 m swimming; 2000 m rowing; any single or repeated sprint lasting ~30 s–12 min.

## Managing the GI side effects of sodium bicarbonate
tags: "sodium-bicarbonate", "baking-soda", bicarb, supplement, safe, dose, timing
Evidence strength: STRONG; strategies are well-documented in the ISSN position stand.

GI distress (bloating, nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting) is the primary barrier to sodium bicarbonate use. Incidence is variable between individuals.

**Mitigation strategies (in order of effectiveness):**
1. Reduce dose to 0.2–0.25 g/kg and build up gradually.
2. Time ingestion ~180 minutes (3 hours) before exercise — buffering still peaks before competition.
3. Consume with a high-carbohydrate meal to slow absorption.
4. Use enteric-coated capsules (slower GI transit; less acute irritation).
5. Use the multi-day loading protocol (0.4–0.5 g/kg/day × 3–7 days) instead of a single acute dose.

**Who this is relevant for:** most useful for competitive athletes in combat sports, rowing, track events, or swimming. Less practical for recreational gym-goers whose training doesn't feature 30-second to 12-minute high-intensity intervals. Experiment in training before race day.
