# Training Frequency & Hypertrophy: Volume Is the Key Variable, Not Frequency

**Full citation:** Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Krieger J. How many times per week should a muscle be trained to maximize muscle hypertrophy? A systematic review and meta-analysis of studies examining the effects of resistance training frequency. J Sports Sci. 2019;37(11):1286–1295. doi:10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906. PMID:30558493.
**Source URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/
**Access:** abstract-only (J Sports Sci is subscription; abstract from PubMed)

## What this source covers
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 25 experimental studies examining weekly resistance training frequency effects on muscle hypertrophy adaptations.

## Key findings & numbers
- When training **volume is equated**, there is **no significant or meaningful difference** in muscle hypertrophy between higher vs lower weekly training frequencies.
- No significant differences across all frequency categories (1×, 2×, 3×+ per week) for direct hypertrophy measures.
- No difference between upper body and lower body training responses to frequency manipulation.
- When volume was **not equated**, higher frequencies showed modest advantages — attributable to the accompanying increase in total volume.
- 25 studies met inclusion criteria.
- Practical recommendation: individuals can select weekly training frequency based on personal preference and scheduling, provided total weekly volume is maintained.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "Resistance training frequency does not significantly or meaningfully impact muscle hypertrophy when volume is equated."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Frequency is a programming tool to manage volume, not a direct driver of hypertrophy.
- Users can train a muscle 2× or 3×/week with identical results if total weekly sets are equal.
- For users with scheduling constraints, a lower frequency (e.g., 1×/week full body) is viable if they hit the same weekly set targets.
- Higher frequency is useful for distributing volume to avoid excessive fatigue per session.
