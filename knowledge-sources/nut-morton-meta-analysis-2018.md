## Protein ceiling for muscle gain — what the meta-analysis says
tags: protein, muscle, gain, build, bulk, mass, how-much, macros, calories
The largest meta-analysis on protein and resistance training (49 RCTs, 1,863 participants) found that protein supplementation significantly increased fat-free mass by **+0.30 kg** and 1-rep-max strength by **+2.49 kg** above training alone. The critical finding: beyond **1.62 g/kg/day**, extra protein produced no further muscle gain. The upper confidence interval of this break point is **2.2 g/kg/day**, making that the practical ceiling for muscle-building contexts. Eating more protein above 2.2 g/kg/day is not harmful, but the muscle-building signal is saturated.

## Protein and age — older adults respond differently
tags: protein, macros, muscle, gain, women, advanced, how-much, goal
The benefit of additional protein for muscle gain decreases with age: the effect was **+0.55 kg fat-free mass** in adults under 45, but only **+0.06 kg** in those over 45. Resistance-trained individuals gained more from added protein (+0.75 kg FFM, p=0.03) than untrained subjects. This does not mean older adults should eat less protein — research elsewhere supports higher per-meal doses (~40 g) for older adults to overcome the blunted anabolic response. The muscle-building ceiling number (1.62 g/kg) applies to surpluses; deficits require higher protein to protect existing muscle.
