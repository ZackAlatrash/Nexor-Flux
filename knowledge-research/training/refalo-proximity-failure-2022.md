# Proximity to Failure & Hypertrophy: Training to Failure Is Not Superior to Non-Failure

**Full citation:** Refalo MC, Helms ER, Trexler ET, Hamilton DL, Fyfe JJ. Influence of Resistance Training Proximity-to-Failure on Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis. Sports Med. 2022. doi:10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y. PMC9935748.
**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9935748/
**Access:** open access (CC BY)

## What this source covers
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 15 studies examining whether resistance training to momentary muscular failure (MMF) produces greater hypertrophy than non-failure training, and whether velocity loss thresholds matter.

## Key findings & numbers
- **Overall (set failure vs non-failure):** Trivial advantage for set failure — ES = 0.19 (95% CI 0.00–0.37), p = 0.045. Requires cautious interpretation.
- **Momentary muscular failure (MMF) vs non-failure (Theme A):** No significant difference — ES = 0.12 (95% CI −0.13–0.37), p = 0.343. "No evidence to support that training to MMF is superior."
- **Velocity loss thresholds (Theme C):**
  - High (>25%) vs Moderate (20–25%): ES = 0.08 (95% CI −0.16–0.32), p = 0.529 — no significant difference.
  - Non-linear pattern: Low (<20% VL): ES = 0.20; Moderate (20–25%): ES = 0.39; High (>25%): ES = 0.42.
- **Load moderation:** Larger effect favoring failure with lower loads (≤50% 1RM) ES = 0.28 vs higher loads (>50% 1RM) ES = 0.15.
- 15 studies total: Theme A (MMF, 5 studies), Theme B (other failure definitions, 4 studies), Theme C (velocity loss, 6 studies).
- TESTEX quality scores: mean 9.9, median 10 (out of 15); 11/15 scored >10.
- Potential non-linear relationship between proximity-to-failure and hypertrophy is suggested.

## Direct specifics / quotes
> "No evidence to support that resistance training performed to momentary muscular failure is superior to non-failure resistance training for muscle hypertrophy."

> "A potential non-linear relationship between proximity-to-failure and muscle hypertrophy."

## Relevance to a body-recomposition coaching app
- Training to failure every set is NOT necessary for hypertrophy — this is a major fatigue saver.
- Leaving 1–3 RIR (reps in reserve) per set appears sufficient for comparable hypertrophy.
- At lower loads (≤50% 1RM), training closer to failure matters more — relevant for bodyweight/BFR training.
- Non-linear relationship: some proximity to failure is needed, but the marginal gain from going to complete MMF is trivial and may increase injury risk and recovery demands.
- For a recomp app: recommend "stop 1–3 reps short of failure" as the default guideline.
